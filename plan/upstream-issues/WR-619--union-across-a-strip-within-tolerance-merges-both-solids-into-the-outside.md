#### WR-619. A union of solids within tolerance of each other across a narrow strip merges both solids into the outside
- **Severity:** Wrong result (a union of two solids returns a body with no solid region, volume 0; reported as success)
- **Area:** Booleans
- **Location:** `source/SMLib/src/SmMerge.cpp`, `SmMerge::ManifoldBoolean` (def. `:2510`), STEP 6 (`m_vTI.DeleteFaces(sDelFaces, ..)`, `:4724`, `SmTopologyIntersector::DeleteFaces` def. `source/SMLib/src/SmTopologyIntersector.cpp:456`) and STEP 7 (`:4782`-`:4898`); the region merge in `SmBrep::DeleteFace` (`source/SMLib/src/SmBrep.cpp:7170`-`:7199`).
- **What happens:** two planar faces of the operands stay within tolerance of each other across a narrow strip without being coincident (they cross at a small angle, so no ProtoFace joins them), while the operands' top and bottom faces are coplanar. STEP 6 deletes two faces of the primary operand A: one on its plane y = 20 (the face that crosses B's F1 in the strip) and one on its bottom plane z = 0 (coplanar with B's bottom face). Each has a solid region on one side and the infinite region on the other, so each `DeleteFace` merges a solid region into the infinite region (`DeleteFace` keeps the infinite or void region of the two it joins): the first A's solid region, the second the region the merge split off when it added B's faces. STEP 7 then marks the one region left solid (`SetIsVoid(FALSE)`, `:4895`), and `FindAndSetInfiniteRegion` makes it the infinite region and void again. The result keeps every face, all of their face uses in one shell of the infinite region, with no solid region: not a manifold solid, volume 0. Why the classification deletes these two faces, where a union deletes only faces inside the other operand, is not traced further here.
- **Trigger:** `SmApiBooleanUnion` of two 4 mm cubes, A `[-2, 2] x [16, 20] x [0, 4]` and B turned atan(0.1) (5.71 deg) about z, whose faces (A's y = 20, B's F1) cross at x = 1.99995 and stay within 5e-6 of each other across x in [1.9999, 2]. The same cubes within 1e-5 across x in [1.9998, 2] fail instead (WR-614). Both operands are valid manifold solids.
- **How found:** tangency-labs/solidcore#1794 (WR-614 follow-up): the union that succeeds a hair from the failing one returns no solid. Traced with gdb on both sides.
- **Confirmed:** yes, on the C++ build. `boolean.api_brep` fixture `fixtures/boolean.api_brep/wr614_coincident_strip.json`, case `wr614_strip_union_d5e-06`, gives the same result on both sides (12 faces, 26 edges, 16 vertices, one region, not a manifold solid). In both engines `ManifoldBoolean`'s STEP 6 deletes two faces, and STEP 7 and `FindAndSetInfiniteRegion` then reset the one region left; in the port (gdb, the deleted faces' planes read from their surfaces) the two `DeleteFace` calls delete A's faces on y = 20 and on z = 0, and each merges a solid region into the infinite region.
- **Rust port:** behaves as upstream by default. Through the fork's C API (`smlib_boolean`) the body is refused with `SMLIB_ERR_NOT_SOLID` (`finish()`, its result check); a direct caller of the port gets the region-less body. With the opt-in `Kernel::fixes.boolean_union_refuses_solid_leak` (own design), `manifold_boolean` refuses a union with `SM_ERR` (the face in the error) when a face its step 6 would delete has the infinite region on one side and a solid region on the other (`boolean/merge/p4.rs`, `face_leaking_solid`). Only a union is checked: a union deletes the faces of each operand that lie inside the other, which have material on both sides, so it never rightly deletes a face between a solid and the outside; an intersection's step 6 does exactly that on purpose (it deletes A's faces outside B, and merging A's solid there into the outside is how that part becomes void). `crates/solidcore/tests/wr_619_union_solid_leak.rs` checks the cubes' union both ways and that an ordinary union of two overlapping boxes is unchanged.
