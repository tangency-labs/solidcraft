#### WR-578. A solid offset leaves a loop's vertex behind where two consecutive edges' offsets run parallel
- **Severity:** Failure (the shell is refused with 1001)
- **Area:** Offsets and shells
- **Location:** `source/SMLib/src/SmOffsetExecutive.cpp`, `SmOffsetExecutive::DoExtendedOffset` (def. `:1105`): nothing between building the offset edges and `SmOffsetExecutive::PiecewiseMerge` (def. `:2362`) closes a face loop whose consecutive edge offsets no longer meet.
- **What happens:** at a vertex where two consecutive edges of a face loop offset to parallel lines (for example a box's side and a cylinder's base, which lie one wall thickness apart after the offset), the offsets never meet. The loop's vertex stays at the end of one edge while the other edge's curve runs elsewhere. In the merge, `SmFace::CreateUVTrimCurves` grows the vertex's tolerance to cover the gap, the imprint lets that vertex capture a neighbour, and `SmMerge::MakeFaceuse` fails, so the shell is refused with 1001.
- **Trigger:** SolidCraft rig case shell/1494: a 47.5 x 45 x 30 box joined with an r7.5 cylinder along x from its side x = 47.5, the axis in the front face's plane y = 0, shelled 1 inward and open at the bottom.
- **How found:** reported by SolidCraft. The fork's fix is tangency-labs/smlib 7ff0a94 ("a solid offset closes a loop's gap where two consecutive edges' offsets run parallel"), and 34318f4 gives a vertex moved past its edge's other end its own status. Neither is merged upstream.
- **Confirmed:** no C++ run was made here. The fork reports 8864.598272772 for the shell (relative difference at most 7e-11 across four seam angles), and 1001 before its fix.
- **Rust port:** behaves as upstream by default. With `Kernel::fixes.offset_closes_loop_gaps`, `do_extended_offset` runs `close_loop_gaps` after the collapsed faces' end edges are squeezed out and before the excess faces are removed.
  - Where a loop's consecutive edge uses meet at a vertex but their ends lie apart, the edge whose end is away from the vertex gets the straight segment to it, joins it and is split there. The segment must lie on the face's extended surface.
  - A vertex that moved into the edge trims the edge instead.
  - A vertex that moved to or past the edge's other end is `SM_ERR_OFFSET_EDGE_VANISHES` (1043): the face would turn inside out.
  - Each face's search is bounded at 64 passes (`SM_ERR` past it).

  `tests/wr_578_offset_closes_loop_gaps.rs` covers both cases. The box-and-cylinder shell, with its seam 30 and 55 degrees off the front face, checks the fork's volume to 1e-8 and is `#[ignore]`d for now. The port shells both sound but measures 8864.600632417863 (seam 30) and 8864.59147077024 (seam 55), 2.7e-7 and 7.7e-7 off the fork's 8864.598272772, where the fork reports 7e-11. That divergence is to be traced.
- **Open in the port (2026-10-30 probe):** with the seam 125 or 200 degrees off the front face, the offset is still no closed solid. Upstream's `ShellBrep` would hand such an offset back as a success (the WR-454 defect), so the flag enforces the refusal itself: with `offset_closes_loop_gaps`, `shell_brep` refuses (`SM_ERR`) an offset of a solid that is not a manifold solid, whether or not WR-454's `shell_empty_cavity_fails` is set. The flag never turns upstream's 1001 into a broken body. `wr_578_other_seams_are_refused` checks both seams with the flag alone. Building the fork's solid at these seams is still open; that test is `#[ignore]`d.
- **Limit, #1528's plate (tangency-labs/solidcore#1528):** with the flag, the plate (a hole through a round side, shelled 2 inward) shells on a clean operand: 16 faces, 36 edges, sound, volume 10400.21299138017. After a prior mass-properties query on the operand it is refused (`SM_ERR` from `close_loop_gaps`). The query writes UV trims, snaps a trim control point (`FixUVTrimCurves`) and fills vertex/face gap caches that the offset reads. That is upstream behaviour, matched by the pinned C++ (WR-583). `wr_578_issue_1528_plate_shells_on_a_clean_operand` checks the clean-operand case. #1528 reports that the fork refuses this plate; whether the fork's run had measured the body first is not known.
