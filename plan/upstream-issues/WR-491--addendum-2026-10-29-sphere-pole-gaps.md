Updates: WR-491

- **Regression in the opt-in fix (#1437):** with `Kernel::fixes.trim_boundary_seam_wrap` on, a primitive sphere measured area, volume and every moment exactly 0, with no error. The sphere's single face has a gap line at each pole (degenerate in u at v = 0 and v = 1), and each of those gaps is exactly one period in u, so `seam_wrap` took both for seam crossings. The two shifts cancel, so the "net shift, fall back" check passed, and the loop that was left went up the seam and straight back down it, enclosing nothing. The C++ (and the flag-off Rust) is right there: a gap line along a singular side is the fill `CreateOrientedTrimBoundary` exists for.
- **Fix (still opt-in, flag-off path unchanged):** before wrapping, `gap_is_singular` evaluates the face's surface along the unshifted UV gap at t = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1. If each point is within twice the face tolerance of the gap's start (the same tolerance `seam_periods` uses for closedness), the gap is a singular side (a pole or an apex) and keeps upstream's line. A real seam crossing goes round the whole surface, so its middle is far from its ends in 3D. The WR-491 frustum is unaffected.
- **Evidence:** `crates/solidcore/tests/trim_boundary_seam_wrap.rs`. At accuracy 1e-6, with the flag on, the full sphere, the upper hemisphere (a sphere minus a box; one pole gap), a 90 degree sphere sector (meets both poles), torus R10 r3 and cylinder r5 h12 each measure the same area and volume as with the flag off, bit for bit, and agree with the exact values to 1e-5. Before the fix, the sphere gave 0/0 with the flag on and 615.75249 / 1436.75508 with it off. At 1e-8, with `precise_properties_body_length_scale` also on, the sphere at (1, -2, 3) gives area, volume and second volume moments (V r^2 / 5 each) to 1e-7 or better. The three WR-491 frustum tests still pass.
