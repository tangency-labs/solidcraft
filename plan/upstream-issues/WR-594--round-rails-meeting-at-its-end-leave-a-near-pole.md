#### WR-594. A circular round whose rails meet at its end gets a near pole instead of an exact one (SmSurface test 5)
- **Severity:** Wrong result
- **Area:** Blends
- **Location:** `source/SMLib/src/SmFilletStandardSolver.cpp`, `SmCircularCrossSectionFSG::CreateSurfaceFromCurves`, after the loop that inserts knots until the rails have at least 4 (def. `:3233` at the pin).
- **What happens:** at a degenerate corner the cross section is a point, and `CreateSurfaceFromCurves` makes that point section at the first rail's end. Each rail is lifted from its own surface (`SmFilletIntersector::FlushCurve`), though, and at a vertex the two surfaces pass through it only to within the model's accuracy. So the rails end where each surface meets the vertex, a few 1e-12 mm apart. The second rail is a boundary of the skinned surface, so the surface's last row keeps that rail's end at one corner and the first rail's end at the others. That is a near pole, not an exact one, and the result's check refuses the surface as close to singular at its boundary (`SmSurface` test 5).
- **Trigger:** SolidCraft's test-peg part: a stadium bar whose top meets a cylinder standing on its round end, the bar's sides tangent to the cylinder, with sketch-solver residues of about 1e-10 mm. The bar's front top edge is rounded r 2, then the concave arc where the top meets the cylinder is rounded r 2 with its tangent chain. The second round ends where the cylinder, the front side and the first round touch at its one fillet vertex, and its rails' ends there are 6.5e-12 mm apart. With exact dimensions the surfaces meet the vertex at one point and the pole is exact.
- **How found:** reported by SolidCraft. The fork's fix is tangency-labs/smlib@5fdefb9 ("fillets: the rails of a round meeting at its end end at one point"), which is not merged upstream. The code is SMLib's own and is unchanged at the pin.
- **Confirmed:** partly. The Rust port, which matches the pinned C++, keeps the second rail's end in the surface: given rails from x = 0 to 10 that meet at (10, 2, 0) to 6.5e-12, `CircularCrossSectionFsg::create_surface_from_curves` returns a surface whose two corners at that end differ by the miss (`crates/solidcore/tests/wr_594_rails_meet_at_one_end_point.rs`). The peg's two-round sequence was not reproduced: the port's second round fails with 1001, or builds a body its check refuses for other reasons, before the branch is reached.
- **Fix:** tangency-labs/smlib@5fdefb9. At each end of the rails' interval where the rails' end points are within the approximation tolerance of each other (the case the point section is made for), both rails' end control points move to the midpoint of the two ends, keeping their weights, before the surface is skinned. The end then collapses exactly.
- **Rust port:** behaves as upstream by default. `Kernel::fixes.fillet_rails_meet_at_one_end_point` ports the fork's change in `CircularCrossSectionFsg::create_surface_from_curves`, after `add_section_knots`. The offset unit's own call of `create_surface_from_curves` is covered by the flag too, as it is the same C++ function.
