# nlib.srf_nurb: retiring `closed_smooth_pole_tangent_limit` (WR-725): the moved rows checked (2026-11-12)

Updates: plan/findings/nlib.srf_nurb--wr725-pole-limit-moves-fifty-golden-rows.md

- **The patch's own moves (measured, swarm run 14246-pole-x1b, main 0371e7d2):** main and main + the retirement, each blessed in its own build, differ in 162 rows in six units: `blend.fillet_solver_p4` 4, `blend.fillet_standard_solver_p1` 128, `blend.fillet_standard_solver_p2` 6, `nlib.srf_adv_p1` 4, `nlib.srf_nurb` 4, `surface.offset_surface` 16. The 312 rows in 21 units where main's own output differs from expected/ are main's drift, not the patch's.
- **Why extensions grow by one domain length (read):** `extend_surface` (`surface/offset_surface.rs`, from line 3201) first extends to the point pushed one domain size farther out (3223, 3264-3280) and only on an error retries at the point itself (3283-3295). `eval_pt_derivs_unbounded` (`nurbs/srf_adv/p1.rs` 340-346) returns the closure test's failed derivative angle as that error. At a pole on the seam upstream's angle of two vanishing vectors fails, so on main every first try at such a surface fails and the retry's shorter extension is used; with the pole limit the first try succeeds. The size is `domain.size()`, a derived quantity; the pole fix changes which of the two designed tries runs, not the size policy.
- **`nlib.srf_nurb` (4 rows, measured against the nets):** gen0652, gen1542, gen1707 become u-closed (gen1707's net is an exact rational sphere of radius 2.287274 about the origin); gen1708 becomes not v-closed (its first row is a pole, but its v-boundary columns are 1.55 apart at u = 1, against a tolerance of 0.001). Each new answer matches the geometry; main returned status 17.
- **The exact 1.0 moves (`blend.fillet_standard_solver_p2`, measured):** they are the extended offset surfaces' domain bounds, one more domain length (1.0) of extension. gen0067's solved values agree to 1 ulp; gen0131 fails (1001) both ways.
- **Periodic direction, `surface.offset_surface` gen0354 (measured):** an offset (d = 0.7557) of a surface of revolution whose generator starts on its axis, evaluated at angle parameter 4.5695, outside the angle domain [0, 1]. The exact offset point at generator parameter 1.0, angle 0, is (2.182469026277505, 0, 2.918854636868159). With the retirement the point is on that surface to 7e-12 (at the seam). On main it is 43.3 off the surface (45.4 from the axis, where the surface's radius is 2.18): a tangent extension of the closed direction. The periodic image of the parameter (204°, assuming the standard rational circle parameterization; inferred) is 4.27 from the retirement's point and 46.5 from main's. The retirement is closer; it is not the periodic image.
- **Open directions, beyond the domain (measured; no exact reference exists there):** an extension past an open boundary is not unique, and its geometry at a point depends on how far it is built.
  - `surface.offset_surface` gen2080: the evaluated point agrees to 1 ulp. What moves is the extended domain (v min -0.4395 to -1.4395, the one-size margin) and the state's grid, which samples that larger domain.
  - `surface.offset_surface` gen1256: the point agrees to 1e-14 relative. The derivatives at the extension's pole row (u = 0, v = -2.904) shrink by a factor of 28 (second derivatives of order 1e10 on both sides: singular there).
  - `blend.fillet_standard_solver_p1` (the three largest rows, 6.5e-5, 1.9e-5, 1.8e-5): a central-difference Jacobian at u = -0.5, past the pole of a quarter-cone patch. Six of its 30 values move: those near zero go from 1e-11 on main to 1e-5 with the retirement, the others (-0.84853, 1.35147, -1.00001) by up to 2e-5 relative; the rest agree to 1e-15.
- **Verdict:** better where a reference exists (the four srf_nurb rows against their nets; gen0354 against the exact surface of revolution), and neutral where none does (open-direction evaluations whose points agree; the moved values are extension-dependent). The other rows were not checked one by one: 7 of the 16 offset_surface rows move by at most 2e-14, and 116 of the 128 standard_solver_p1 rows by at most 1e-6.
