#### WR-755. The merge reads two faces as coincident when they only share a tangent edge, so a fillet against a fillet a hair apart is classified on the wrong side
- **Severity:** Wrong result (open or invalid body, or status 1001, on a boolean of two nearly coincident filleted bodies)
- **Area:** Boolean (merge, face coincidence)
- **Location:** at the pin `3fd5b95`: `source/SMLib/src/SmMerge.cpp:2129` (`FixFoundFaceCoincidence`) and the radial-sector search it relies on. A mated edge's radial sector compares the two faces only at the edge, and the faces of the other body found coincident there are taken as coincident faces.
- **What happens:** two fillets (or cones, spheres, tori) tangent at a mated edge but parting further along the surface, by up to the shift between the bodies, read as coincident. The face found "coincident" at the edge gets the other side's region, so the result is not closed or the boolean fails.
- **Trigger:** two 40 x 30 x 10 boxes with R2 vertical fillets, the second shifted by dx along x, both A - B and B - A. dx = 1e-4 and 1e-3 fail in both orders; dx = 1e-2 fails in both orders; dx = 0.1 works.
- **How found:** issue #3012 (a near-coincident cut of filleted bodies).
- **Confirmed:** by reading the C++ at the pin (`source/SMLib/src`): `SmMerge::ManifoldBoolean` step 7.2 calls `pOtherEdge->FindRadialSector(pEU, ...)` (`SmMerge.cpp:3207`; the mirror for the other body's edge uses is at `SmMerge.cpp:3447`). `SmEdge::FindRadialSector` (`SmEdge.cpp:1789`) takes cases 2 to 5 of `RadialSectorClassify` as coincident and returns the coincident face use (`SmEdge.cpp:1884-1897`). `RadialSectorClassify` decides coincidence from the step-off binormals at the one edge: a minimum distance steady within 1% over three steps, or a binormal angle under 0.01 degrees (`SmEdge.cpp:1610-1625`), held for eight consecutive steps (`SmEdge.cpp:1631-1639`); its own comment says "it may be possible for this check to go wrong in either direction" (`SmEdge.cpp:1608`). Tangent fillets parting along the surface give such binormals at their shared edge. The caller takes any returned face use as a coincident face (`if (pFoundFU)`, `SmMerge.cpp:3220`; mirror `SmMerge.cpp:3463`) and hands it to `FixFoundFaceCoincidence` (`SmMerge.cpp:2129`, called at `SmMerge.cpp:3258` and `SmMerge.cpp:3496`), which only checks that the faces' edges are mated: no step compares the surfaces away from the edge. The Rust port has the same chain.
- **Rust port:** `p1::faces_coincide` gates the coincident-surface merge in `classify_edge_faces` and the coincident face uses in `find_brep_region_of_other_face`: a face counts as coincident only when its surface does, at the larger of the two face tolerances, except for zero-angle sectors (`RadialSector::zero_sector`). Gated: cone (a cylinder is a cone of semi-angle 0), sphere and torus pairs, pairs of one kind, decided from their definitions (`Surface::same_analytic_surface`, not the sampled `coincidence_check`): cones by coaxial lines (either sense), and radii within the tolerance at both ends of the two faces' box along the axis; spheres by centre and radius; tori by axis, centre and both radii; a torus with no hole (major radius within the tolerance of 0) is a sphere. Exempt (the sector decides): two planes (its step-off measures their gap exactly), two different kinds (tangent, they can stay within the tolerance over a patch: a sphere cap thinner than it on a box face, #2412), and any kind with no definition to compare (offset, B-spline, extrusion, revolution): with the sampled check on offset against offset, the golden case `round_merge_deleted_surface_user_problemShell15_face8_inset` moves (it reads its faces as apart). dx = 1e-4, 1e-3: valid, volume dx * 300 less the tapered ends, both orders; dx = 1e-2: B - A valid, A - B has the right volume but fails `check` with a Vertex/Face gap across an Edgeuse (offset equal to the tolerance at 8 digits), a separate cause not fixed here. The fix needs `linear_sweep_arc_is_cylinder` and `classify_edge_interval` (without the first the extruded body's fillets are surfaces of extrusion, which the gate leaves alone; without the second the result is not a closed solid); SolidCraft runs both. Test: `crates/solidcore-ops/tests/issue_3012_filleted_boxes_apart.rs`.
