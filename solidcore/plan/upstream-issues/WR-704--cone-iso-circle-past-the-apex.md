#### WR-704. `SmCone::CreateIsoCircleFromPoint` fails for a point within tolerance past a cone's apex end, and `SmSphere::IntersectWithCone` with it
- **Severity:** Wrong result
- **Area:** Surfaces and volumes
- **Location:** `source/SMLib/src/SmCone.cpp:1679`, `SmCone::CreateIsoCircleFromPoint` (def. `:1620`); its caller `source/SMLib/src/SmSphere.cpp:2601`, `SmSphere::IntersectWithCone` (def. `:2381`).
- **What happens:** `CreateIsoCircleFromPoint` accepts a reference point whose projection onto the axis, `dParam`, is within `d3DTolerance` of the height interval. It moves the point onto the cone at that height (`GetRadius(dParam)` along the point's direction) and inverts it for the circle's STEP height. On a cone whose apex is an end (bottom radius 0), a `dParam` below the apex gives a negative radius, so the moved point is on the far nappe. `STEPInversion` mirrors it through the apex, inverts it there and flips the height back. It succeeds with a height outside the interval (`dParam` itself), and `CreateIsoParametricCurve` at that height fails with `SM_ERR`. `IntersectWithCone` passes such a point when a sphere centred on the axis crosses the cone's line within `dTol` past the apex (its `adLineInt[i] < 0` branch), and `SER` fails the whole intersection.
- **Trigger:** the cone apex (0, 0, 0), Z axis, semi-angle 70, heights 0 to 1 (bottom radius 0, top radius tan 70). A point at height −5e-4 with tolerance 1e-3, on, inside or outside the cone or on its axis, gives `SM_ERR`. A sphere centred at (0, 0, 2) through the point 2e-3 past the apex along the cone's line, intersected with tolerance 1e-3, gives `SM_ERR`; the line's other crossing, the circle at height 4 cos² 70 + 2e-3 cos 70, is lost with it. Past the far end of the same cone, or past either end of a cone with no apex at an end, the inversion fails instead and the port's UB-87 handling builds the end circle.
- **How found:** a probe of the SolidCraft fork's tangency-labs/smlib 1279539. The fork sets the circle's STEP height to `dParam` clamped to the height interval and drops the inversion. For the fork, this case is a side effect: it fixed the far end, where the inversion leaves the height unset (UB-87 here).
- **Confirmed:** in the port (`crates/solidcore/tests/fork_1279539_cone_iso_circle_at_axial_height.rs`). The C++ was not run: the port follows its mirror and flip line for line, so the pinned C++ is expected to fail the same way.
- **Rust port:** opt-in `Kernel::fixes.cone_iso_circle_at_axial_height`, off by default. With it on, `CreateIsoCircleFromPoint` is the fork's at 1279539:
  - the STEP point (u min, `dParam` clamped to the height interval), with no inversion;
  - a failed `ConvertUVFromSTEPToNURBS` returned, the fork's `SER` from 672a437, where upstream reads the value the polar converter wrote.
- **What the flag changes** (batches 54j1 and 54j255). The flag was forced on for every call in `surface.cone_p1`, `surface.cone_p2`, `surface.cone_p3`, `surface.sphere`, `surface.surf_of_extrusion_p2`, `surface.surf_of_revolution_p3`, `intersect.api_intersectors` and `surface.surface_p2`.
  - 345 cases change their Rust output. Most of them change in the last bits only: the clamped `dParam` against the inverted height.
  - 116 differ from the pinned C++ beyond the 1e-9 gate:
    - **52 (`surface.cone_p1`): a point within the tolerance past an end gets that end's circle.** This is the fork's rule. In 45 of them upstream fails with `SM_ERR`, as in the trigger above: the inversion lands outside the interval, mirrored through an apex or past the end of a cone built out of bounds. These include the 9 apex cases of the trigger. In the other 7, upstream builds the circle at the out-of-interval height instead.
    - **30 (`surface.cone_p1`): unbounded cones of semi-angle 5e-11 and 5.7e-11 degrees with swapped parameters.** Upstream's circle, built from the inverted STEP point, is far from the point's height: at 3.95 for a point at height 0.7. The fork's circle, from the sweep start and the clamped height, is at the point's height, to within the NURB's height mapping on these near-cylinders (8.8e-5). **The fork's rule, and here the fork's circle is the right one.** The `SER` on the conversion (above) is not reached on these inputs: the conversion of the sweep start succeeds.
    - **13 (`surface.cone_p1`): unbounded cones of negative semi-angle (−20 and −30 degrees).** The fork's circle is at the point's axial height divided by the cosine of the semi-angle (10.64 for a point at height 10 on the −20 degree cone), not at the point's height. Upstream's inverted height gives a different circle. This is the fork's rule as written. Its STEP height is not the axial distance on these cones. The unbounded constructor rotates the generator only for a positive semi-angle. This is noted for the fork and not changed here, since the flag is the fork's.
    - **10 (`intersect.api_intersectors`, `ssi-sphere-cone-near-pole-*`):** a sphere of radius 5e5 about a cone's base crossing the cone's line within the tolerance of its tip (top radius 0.001 to 0.3). The fork's end circle replaces upstream's. The recorded values that move are of order 1e-2 to 0.3 on a model of size 5e5.
    - **11: rounding.**
      - 8 in `surface.cone_p1` and 1 in `surface.sphere` (`isect_cone_apex_on_sphere`) are values below 1e-9 in magnitude that differ by at most 5.6e-16, such as 2.2e-16 against −2.2e-16. They fail only the relative test.
      - 2 more in `surface.cone_p1` are on an unbounded cone of semi-angle 89 degrees, at 3.5e-9 relative.
  - **Tests:** `crates/solidcore/tests/fork_1279539_cone_iso_circle_at_axial_height.rs`.
