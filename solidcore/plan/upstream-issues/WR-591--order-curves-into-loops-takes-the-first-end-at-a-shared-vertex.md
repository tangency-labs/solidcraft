#### WR-591. `OrderCurvesIntoLoops` goes on from the first curve end by index where loops touch at a vertex, so the input order decides whether the loops close
- **Severity:** Wrong result
- **Area:** Topology
- **Location:** `source/SMLib/src/SmTrimmingTools.cpp:1961` (`SmTools::FindFirstPointWithinTolToPnt`, then `nFoundIndex` taken as the next curve), `SmTrimmingTools::OrderCurvesIntoLoops` (def. `:1739`).
- **Pinned reference:** SMLib v1.0.1, commit `3fd5b95`.
- **What happens:** Where several curve ends meet at one point (a hole whose corner touches the outline), the loop goes on along the first end there by index in the input list. So the list's order decides whether a loop closes, ends on a curve it already used (`SM_ERR`), or runs round the hole the wrong way, crossing itself at the vertex and counting the hole as material. In SolidCraft a flat sheet with a cut-out whose corner touches a bend line's end extruded to (area + 2) instead of (area - 2).
- **Trigger:** `OrderCurvesIntoLoops` (tolerance `1e-6`) of a 10 x 6 outline through (0, 3) on its left side and the triangle (0, 3), (2, 3.5), (2, 2.5), as eight lines, in each of the 8 rotations of the list with neither, the outline or the hole reversed (24 orders): 15 of them fail or give loops other than areas {60, 1} or {59}.
- **How found:** SolidCraft (engine sheet test `a_cut_out_with_a_corner_on_a_bend_line_refolds`); fixed in the fork as tangency-labs/smlib@9209e76 ("loops: where loops touch at a vertex, go on round the vertex").
- **Confirmed:** yes, in the port at parity with the pinned C++ (default off).

The port keeps the pinned result by default. With the opt-in, default-off `Kernel::fixes.loops_go_on_round_the_vertex` (`crates/solidcore/src/kernel.rs`), `order_curves_into_loops` (`crates/solidcore/src/topology/trimming_tools.rs`) calls `next_round_the_vertex` (the fork's `sm_NextRoundTheVertex`) after the point search: of the ends at the vertex it can go on from (curves not yet used, and the loop's first curve's start), it takes the one whose leaving tangent comes next round the vertex from the arriving curve's, about the plane the tangents span, ordering ends that leave tangent to each other by how they bend. `crates/solidcore/tests/wr_591_loops_touching_at_a_vertex.rs` checks all 24 orders close soundly with the fix on, the 15 upstream failures with it off, and that loops touching nowhere are ordered the same either way.
