#### WR-781. `SmBrep::ProjectAndTrim` gives a face the curve does not cut through to the side with more corners
- **Severity:** Wrong result
- **Area:** Topology, trimming
- **Location:** `source/SMLib/src/SmBrep.cpp:25474` (the majority of the votes) and `:25477` (the tie test), in `SmBrep::ProjectAndTrim` (def. `:25278`); `:25390` and `:25483` for the list of edges.
- **What happens:** After the projected curve is imprinted, each face is given a side of the curve. Its vertices vote (`sm_FindSideOfCurve` for each; a vertex on the curve, or one whose projection fails, has no vote). With no vote the mid-points of its edges vote. The side with more votes wins. Only when the votes are equal is the face judged by a point: the point of the face nearest a ray through the reference point along the projection direction.

  A face that the curve cuts in two becomes two faces, each with votes on one side only. A face with votes on both sides is therefore one that the curve does not cut through: the curve ends inside it, or passes beside it. For such a face the majority is only a count of corners. A face with one corner left of the curve and three right of it is taken to be right of the curve, also when the reference point lies on the face left of the curve. `SM_TT_KEEP_POINT` then deletes the face that the reference point is on.

  The same code builds the shape for the ray test from `sEdges`, which is filled only for a face that had no vertex vote. For a face with tied vertex votes the list still holds the edges of an earlier face, or none.
- **Trigger:** the planar face with corners (0,0), (1,0), (1.2,0.1), (1.2,0.3), (1,1), (0,1), the segment from (0.5,-1,1) to (0.5,0.5,1) projected along Z, the reference point (0.25,0.25,0), `SM_TT_KEEP_POINT`. The segment ends inside the face. The votes are one left ((0,0)) and three right; the corners at y = 1 are beyond the end of the segment and have no vote. Measured on the port before the change, which followed the C++ line for line here: the face is deleted and the B-rep is left empty. The square with corners (0,0), (1,0), (1,1), (0,1) has one vote on each side and is kept.
- **How found:** tangency-labs/solidcraft#13697, on the port.
- **Confirmed:** by reading the C++ (the lines above) and on the port. Solidcore judges a face with votes on both sides as it judges a tie, by its point nearest the reference point, and builds the shape for that test from the face's own edges. Test `a_face_the_curve_ends_inside_is_kept_with_the_reference_point` (`topology::brep::p10`). All 283 golden units give the same results, except three cases of `primitive.api_trim_ops` (`gen0049`, `gen0086`, `gen0156`). In each of the three an arc ends inside a face, the face is not cut in two, and the reference point lies on it; the face had one vote on the reference point's side and two on the other.
  - `gen0086` and `gen0156` (`KeepPoint`, a box): the top and the bottom of the box were deleted, and 2 faces were left. They are now kept: 4 faces.
  - `gen0049` (`DeletePoint`, one face): the face was kept. It is now deleted, and the result has no face.
- **Not covered:** what a trim should do when the curve ends inside a face is still a choice (tangency-labs/solidcraft#13697 lists three: keep the face whole, cut it along an extension of the curve, or refuse). This change keeps the first and removes its dependence on the count of corners. A face whose every vote is on one side is judged as before, also when it reaches past the end of the curve.
