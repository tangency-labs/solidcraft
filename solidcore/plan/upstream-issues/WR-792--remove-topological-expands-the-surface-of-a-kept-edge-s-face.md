#### WR-792. Removing topological edges expands a kept edge's face's surface and leaves its UV domain on the old parameterisation
- **Severity:** Wrong result
- **Area:** Topology clean-up with a keep list (fillets, offsets, stitching)
- **Location:** `SmBrep::RemoveTopologicalEdgesAndVertices` (`source/SMLib/src/SmBrep.cpp:25982`, the port's `topology::brep::p5::remove_topological_with_notifications`): every edge is tested with `SmEdge::IsTopological` before the optional keep list is read. The test's coverage step (`CoverCoincidentSurface`, the port's `topology::edge::cover_other_coincident_surface`) expands the covering face's surface in place and removes that face's UV trims; only the merge that follows a removable edge sets the merged face's domain. A kept edge is skipped after the test, so its face keeps the expanded surface under its old UV domain.
- **What happens:**
  - Two faces on one cylinder, split along an edge the caller keeps: the test expands one face's surface onto both. A cylinder kept on its NURBS form gets a new NURBS for the larger surface, with another parameter: a quarter turn that ran over 0 to 1 now runs over about 0 to 3, while the face's domain still says 0 to 1.
  - The face's trims are gone and are dropped again from its edges; the drops leave the domain and fail, and the last resort (`brute_force` in `CreateUVTrimCurve`) samples the edge, clamps every sample past a third of the arc onto the domain's edge, and fits the trim on a third of the edge's interval.
- **Trigger:** tangency-labs/solidcraft#17616: a sheet-metal part with a side flange split into an 8 mm and a 50 mm segment (their bends are two faces on one cylinder each); an R10 round on the 50 mm tab's top outside edge overflows the 3 mm top, and the crease trim's clean-up (`trim_at_creases`, "faces sewn on one surface are one face") keeps every edge it did not sew, among them the edge between the two bends. In solidcore-ops the round fails as not closed; in SolidCraft its result is refused by the export ("a UV trim on [0, 0.33], its edge on [0, 1]").
- **How found:** reported by SolidCraft; traced in the port with the faces' domains and their surfaces' natural domains before and after each step of the crease trim.
- **Confirmed:** in the port, measured in solidcore (no C++ run; the order of the two tests is read from the pinned source): before the clean-up both bends' domains are their surfaces' natural domains (0 to 1 by -35 to 0); after it one bend's surface runs over 0 to 8.01 by -35.7 to 35 and the other's, its u and v swapped by the stitch, over -35.7 to 35 by 0 to 3.2, under their old domains.
- **Fix in solidcore:** an edge on the keep list is not tested: it stays, so its faces are not prepared for a merge. The round builds a closed solid of 45 faces, 93 edges and 50 vertices at the closed-form volume, every bend on its own surface.
