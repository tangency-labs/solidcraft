Updates: WR-56

#### WR-56 (update, 2026-11-16). `good_drop_result_distances_in_order` is removed

`sm_GoodDropResult` now always takes a local result as the foot only when the gap's components along the unit tangents sum under the local solve's tight tolerance `SM_EFF_ZERO (1 + max|P|)`, and it refuses an interior result with either component over that tolerance (the fork's tangency-labs/smlib@eaa998a). Its last test no longer compares distances (see the first bullet). The flag is gone. SolidCraft ran with it on, so the app's drops change only where the first bullet and the exact-tie rule below say. Removed: the field `UpstreamFixes::good_drop_result_distances_in_order`, SolidCraft's preset line, its entry in `fixes.json` and the `issue_2260` flag list, and every test setting of it.

- **The last test takes only a result at its guess; a moved result goes to the global solve, and is kept when that finds nothing as near (#17773).**
  - Upstream's last test refuses a result farther from the point than its guess, with the two distances' names swapped: it took a result farther than its guess and sent one nearer to the global solve. The flag measured them in order, so it took a nearer one.
  - Neither order shows a moved result is the nearest point. With the flag on, a guessed drop kept local minima the global solve beats: goldens `heal.gap` gen0398 sample 26 (a foot 6.9e-6 farther than the nearest point) and `surface.surface_p8` gen0378 (blend samples 63 to 81, feet up to 0.19 farther). Main took farther results.
  - Sending every moved result to the global solve is not enough either: the global solve misses some feet on the domain's boundary (`surface.surface_p5` gen0558: no answer, where the local foot 3.2e-5 away is certified the nearest point) or returns a farther one (gen0958).
  - So `drop_result_verdict` returns `MovedFoot` for a result that passes every other test but moved from its guess (`good_drop_result` stays its `Foot` test), and `DropPoint` runs the global solve and returns the moved foot when the global solve found nothing, or when the answer the drop picks from the global solve (with a guess, the solution nearest the guess in parameters; the whole array, when solutions are asked for and there are several) has no smaller solution value. A drop's answer is then the nearer of the two answers main and the flag returned. This is for intersect, minimise and normalise drops, whose solution value is the distance; for the other operations the global solve decides alone.
  - Still open in #17773: a result at its guess is taken without the global solve, and can be a local minimum too (`heal.gap` gen0398 samples 27 to 29, as on main).
- **The left-out case, goldens `surface.surface_p5` gen0494, is a correct refusal.**
  - The case is an `SM_SO_INTERSECT` drop, from a guess, of a point 7.6e-5 from an apex (a whole row of poles) and 3.36e-5 off the surface. The intersection tolerance is 2e-5.
  - The local solve stops at the foot next to the apex. There `|S_v|` is 7.7e-5, so the gap's component along the unit `S_v` is 5.7e-11, over the tight tolerance 2.5e-12. The drop therefore goes on to the global solve, which finds no intersection.
  - The same drop without a guess gives the same answer on every path. Upstream's 1e-4 foot test turned the foot into a successful intersection only when the drop had that guess.
  - The refusal comes from the tight foot test, because the foot is next to a pole. A local intersect result is still never compared with the drop's tolerance, so a tightly converged foot farther than the tolerance away from a pole is still returned. That is a separate defect, tracked by its own solidcore issue.
  - A convex-hull bound over the surface's Bezier patches shows that every point of the surface is at least 3.3e-5 from the point, so no intersection exists within 2e-5.
  - A minimise drop from the same guess still returns the foot, at the distance the global solve finds.
  - An alternative was measured and rejected: taking the local solve's own "converged" verdict as the foot test, which there includes `InvertPointNearSingularity`. It kept gen0494's success, but it accepted a point 0.342 from the target in place of the 0.0061 foot (gen1376, a minimise drop next to another apex).
- **A result at its guess is taken.** Upstream measures the guess point with `EvaluatePoint` and the result point with `Evaluate1stDerivatives`, and the two can round one ulp apart. With the distances in order, that refused a local result at the guess itself, whose distance came out 1.4e-17 larger (goldens `surface.surface_p7` gen1133). A result at its guess (equal parameters) is now taken without measuring again (a result that does not evaluate is refused before this test), so such a tie no longer depends on `EvaluatePoint`'s rounding or status.
- **Results:** the goldens rows that move, and their attribution by leave-one-out, are in the pull request.
- **Tests:** `surface::surface::p7::tests::issue_1852_good_drop_compares_the_actual_guess_distance` now asserts that every moved result is a `MovedFoot` the last test does not take (it asserted the flag took a nearer one). The goldens certificates (`wr56_*` in `crates/goldens`) enclose the moved rows' drop answers with a certified lower bound. `surface::surface::p7::tests::wr56_a_result_at_its_guess_is_not_farther_than_it` covers gen1133. `surface::surface::p7::tests::wr56_intersect_drop_from_a_guess_answers_as_without_one` covers gen0494: the refusal with and without the guess, the minimise drop's foot, and the distance bound. The three existing WR-56 unit tests keep their fixed-path assertions. `a_drop_from_a_guess_does_not_take_a_stalled_local_solve` now compares the drop with the stalled result's distance directly.
