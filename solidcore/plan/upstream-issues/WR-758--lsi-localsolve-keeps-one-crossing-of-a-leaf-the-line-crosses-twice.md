#### WR-758. `SmLSIGlobalSolver::LocalSolve` keeps one crossing of a leaf the line crosses twice
- **Severity:** Wrong result
- **Area:** Intersection (line/surface), point classification
- **Location:** `source/SMLib/src/SmSurface.cpp` (`SmLSIGlobalSolver::LocalSolve`: the guess loop, its test for ray hits behind the start, and the normal-map test after a kept crossing).
- **What happens:** A leaf is solved from one set of guesses: the middle of the leaf with the middle of the line interval, and further guesses only when that one does not drop onto the leaf. A crossing behind a ray's start is skipped (`continue`) before the polar box test and before the search for a second crossing next to the first. Where a leaf's normal map has a direction perpendicular to the line, the line can cross the leaf twice. When Newton converges to the crossing behind the start, the crossing ahead is never searched for. That search would also look only next to the first crossing, not at a crossing elsewhere in the leaf. A ray that leaves a solid through such a leaf then hits nothing, and `Point3DClassify` puts a point inside the solid in the infinite region.
- **Trigger:** A ray, or any line, that crosses one leaf of a surface's tree twice. Example: a +x classification ray from just inside a cylinder whose spline spans start away from the top of the circle (SolidCraft's extruded circle, seam at 0.6131 rad, spans 35.1 to 125.1 deg): the line just under the top crosses the leaf from 80.1 to 102.6 deg on either side of 90 deg.
- **How found:** SolidCraft #12539: an R1 fillet beside a 1 mm chamfer was refused as cutting the part, because the middle of the round's section, 0.08 mm inside an R30.475 wall, classified as outside (solidcore #3120).
- **Confirmed:** yes, on the port (`surface.surface_p10` matches the C++ bit for bit in difftest). `crates/solidcore-ops/tests/classify_point_grazing_ray.rs`: 57 of 366 points beside the wall classified wrongly before the fix, none after.
- **Rust port:** Fixed. A leaf whose normal map has a perpendicular to the line is halved until each part's has none, so each part holds at most one crossing (Sederberg and Meyers' normal-cone condition). Each part is solved from its own guesses. Parts the line misses, or reaches only behind a ray's start, are dropped. A leaf without such a direction is solved as upstream solves it.
