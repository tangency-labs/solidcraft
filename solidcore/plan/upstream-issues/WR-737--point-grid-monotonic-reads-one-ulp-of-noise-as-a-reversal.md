#### WR-737. `SmPointGrid::SetUpMonotonic` reads one ulp of noise along a collapsed row as a reversal

- **Severity:** Wrong result
- **Area:** Surfaces and volumes
- **Location:** `source/SMLib/src/SmPointSet.cpp:2240` and `:2302`, `SmPointGrid::SetUpMonotonic` (def. `:2179`); reached from `source/SMLib/src/SmBSplineSurface.cpp:5931`, `SmBSplineSurface::AssertValid` test #5. Rust `surface::surface::assert_valid::grid_has_non_monotonic_points`.
- **What happens:** The monotonicity pass walks each row and column of the grid and reports a non-monotonic grid where two neighbouring differences turn back by more than 170 degrees. It skips a pair only when the difference is shorter than `SM_EFF_ZERO` in absolute terms (`dLenSq < SM_EFF_ZERO_SQ`, 1e-12). One ulp of a coordinate is above 1e-12 past about 8192 mm from the origin, so a collapsed or repeated row of control points that carries one-ulp noise there is taken for a zigzag of real steps that reverses at every point, and a surface the grid represents to rounding is refused. The grid is given the distance its points are distinct at (`dTol3d`, `SmPointGrid::SmPointGrid` `:1983`); `SetUpMonotonic` ignores it, and `AssertValid` passes `dTol * 1000` (1e-3) for it. Tests #2 and #3 of the same `AssertValid` recognise coincident points by `dTol` (`SM_ZONE_TOL_3D / 10`). The source notes the gap at `:2239` ("gwc: could look for nonmonotonicity accross coincident CPts").
- **Trigger:** A sphere's NURB is a rational 9 x 5 net whose pole columns are collapsed: all nine points of each are the pole, and consecutive points of such a column are exactly equal, so their zero-length steps are skipped. Moving the net through its homogeneous control points (`[x w + w t, w]`, what a rigid move of a record does) divides by `w` again, which rounds differently for `w = 1` and `w = sqrt(2) / 2`. `create_sphere((0, 0, 5), 2.5)` moved by `t = (1e5, -7e4, 3e4)` has a north pole column whose Euclidean z alternates `30007.5, 30007.500000000004, ...`: steps of 3.638e-12, one ulp of 3e4, reversing at every point. Test #5 then reports 180.0-degree turns at control points i = 0..2 through 6..8 of that column, and `check_brep` refuses the body (`SmBSplineSurface::Test #5 Bad NonMonotonic CPts`, owner `SmSphere`). The same operands at `t = 0` and `t = (1e3, ...)` pass. Reported from SolidCraft's `boolean_props::translation_invariant` (tangency-labs/solidcore#2560), a nightly Rust run reduced to one surface; the Rust port of the pass is faithful here, so the pinned C++ reads the net the same way.
- **How found:** adversarial / edge-case fixture (SolidCraft's `boolean_props::translation_invariant`).
- **Confirmed:** no. The Rust port of `SetUpMonotonic` and of `AssertValid` test #5 reproduces it (`issue_2560_one_ulp_noise_along_a_pole_row_is_not_a_reversal` in `crates/solidcore/src/surface/surface/assert_valid.rs`); the C++ reference was not run on this input.
- **Construction change:** `AssertValid` test #5 calls `grid_has_non_monotonic_points_to_rounding`, which takes a step for no step when it is within the rounding of the two control points it is between, instead of upstream's absolute `SM_EFF_ZERO`. The bound is the one the layer split's pole classifier certifies (`eval::collapse`, solidcore#2629): `Hom::euclid` returns a Euclidean point `P = x / w` with a bound `e = (sqrt(3) ex + |P| ew) / (|w| - ew) + 2 u |P|` on its rounding, and a step is zero when `|P1 - P0| <= e0 + e1 + gamma_3 (|P0| + |P1|)`, the last term the subtraction's own rounding. A net read off a surface (`GetControlPointNet`) divides the stored homogeneous points, exact as stored, so `ex = ew = 0`, `e = 2 u |P|` per point, and the bound is `(2 u + gamma_3) (|P0| + |P1|)`: 1.1e-15 at coordinates of 1, 1.1e-10 at 1e5. It grows with the coordinates, so it is the same statement at every distance from the origin, and the weight is in it through `|P| = |x| / |w|` (a sphere's pole rows carry `w = sqrt(2) / 2`). A width fixed in millimetres was the first attempt and is wrong: `SM_ZONE_TOL_3D / 10` would hide a net whose steps really are 1e-7 mm. A step of real length is unaffected: the planar G2 Setback patches of `plan/findings/blend.blend_corner--planar-g2-setback-patch-net-doubles-back.md` turn back between steps of 3.263e-2 and 5.286e-1 and are still refused, and so is a reversal of 1e-7 mm (`issue_2560_a_reversal_of_a_tenth_of_a_micron_is_refused`). This is solidcore's result for a B-spline surface, with no `Kernel::fixes` flag (AGENTS.md section 4b after solidcore#2647: a fix changes the default). The other caller of `grid_has_non_monotonic_points` (the curved Setback fill's net certificate, solidcore#2366) keeps the absolute `SM_EFF_ZERO`; its own criterion is solidcore#2243's question. Once solidcore#2629 is in, the bound becomes a call of its shared `collapse::gamma` and `Hom`.
- **Difference from the C++, both ways:** the two criteria part where the per-pair bound `(2 u + gamma_3) (|P0| + |P1|)` crosses `SM_EFF_ZERO`, at `|P0| + |P1| = 1801.44` mm. Farther out solidcore passes over a step upstream reads as a direction (the sphere of this entry: one ulp of 3e4 is 3.638e-12, the bound 3.332e-11), so solidcore accepts nets `SetUpMonotonic` refuses. Nearer the origin the bound is below 1e-12 and solidcore is the stricter of the two: a step between the rounding of its own endpoints and `SM_EFF_ZERO` -- 1.110e-15 to 1e-12 at coordinates of 1 -- still carries a direction here, where upstream passes over it, so solidcore can refuse a net `SetUpMonotonic` accepts. No `cpp_defect` marker is added and no case is parked: this entry is **Confirmed: no** (the C++ was never run on such a net), so the C++ result is not verified wrong, and parking needs that (ND-492 is the same situation). The unit's 2370 difftest cases were not re-run against the C++ for this change (the difftest is a diagnostic pinned to usd-brep v1.0.1), so whether any of them now differs is unmeasured. solidcore's own suite was run with the default changed and no test's result moved: the same 4731 test lines over 421 binaries in both runs, 4690 passed, 0 failed, 41 ignored each.
- **Second half of the change, for a step that carries no direction:** upstream replaces the last difference with the skipped one (`vec0 = vec1`, `:2254`, `:2316`), so the step after a skipped one is skipped too. At an absolute `SM_EFF_ZERO` that costs nothing where the coordinates are small, but it hides a real reversal separated by a step within rounding: along x `0, 1, 1 + one ulp, 0` the whole 180-degree turn over steps of 1 passes, although the same net with a separator of 5e-7 is refused. A step within rounding carries no direction, so the pass over it keeps the last difference that had one, in both traversal directions — the gap the source's own note at `:2239` names ("gwc: could look for nonmonotonicity accross coincident CPts"). Pinned by `issue_2560_a_reversal_after_a_step_of_rounding_is_refused`. Short steps are not summed: a run of them is passed over as a whole, so a turn built from several steps each within rounding is still accepted, as upstream accepts it.
- **Not fixed here:** the source of the noise. A rigid move that divides by `w` again cannot keep a collapsed row bit-identical; solidcore's own `N_TranslateCPt` can, because it adds `t` to the Euclidean point and re-weights. The check is left robust to one-ulp noise from any source (a conversion, a solver), as #2560 asks.
