#### WR-767. `SmProtoTopologyManager::CreateProtoEdge` trims the 3D curve and its UV curves in turn with one interval, so the new edge's interval can end past its 3D curve

- **Severity:** Wrong result (the Boolean's result fails `SmEdge::AssertValid` test 2, "Bad Domain")
- **Area:** Boolean
- **Location:** at v1.0.1 (`3fd5b9548d19aa8c12c29bc9637a96d42a06f8a5`):
  - `source/SMLib/src/SmProtoTopology.cpp`, `SmProtoTopologyManager::CreateProtoEdge` (`:4234`-`4299`). The copy of the 3D curve is trimmed with `sCurveDomain` (`:4255`), then each UV curve with the same `sCurveDomain` (`:4264`, `:4273`), each call commented "may snap sIvl by tol to existing knots". The proto edge and its edge definition take `sCurveDomain` as the last trim left it (`:4277`).
  - `source/SMLib/src/SmBSplineCurveNLib.cpp`, `SmBSplineCurve::Trim` (`:4469`). It snaps an end within `100 * SmTol::GetScaledZero` onto a knot of its own curve (`:4541`-`4561`) and writes the snapped ends back to its argument (`:4593`).
  - `source/SMLib/src/SmEdge.cpp`, `SmEdge::AssertValid` test 2 (`:7508`): `m_vInterval.IsContainedBy(m_pCurve->GetNaturalInterval(), SM_EFF_ZERO)`.
- **What happens:** The 3D curve is cut at the classified interval's ends. A UV curve whose own knot lies within 100 scaled zeros of an end moves that end onto its knot, and the edge keeps the moved end. Where the UV curve's knot lies outside the 3D curve's cut end by more than `SM_EFF_ZERO`, the edge's interval runs past its curve's natural interval.
- **Trigger:** A rod cut by two one-turn screw sweeps of one channel profile, one of each hand, starting on the rod's top face: tangency-labs/solidcore#3214, from SolidCraft's tangency-labs/solidcraft#13462. The profile's radial walls meet the rod's cylinder, so each sweep's rail there is a helix on it, and the two rails cross. The crossing is found from more than one face pair, a few 1e-12 apart in the rail's parameter; the second cut's UV curve already holds a knot from an earlier split at one of those values. In the Rust port, the 3D rail is cut at t = 3.32525827878329050, and the UV curve's trim snaps the interval to its knot at 3.32525827878092617. The new edge's interval [3.325258278780926, 2π] then starts 2.4e-12 before its curve's natural interval [3.325258278783290, 2π], which test 2 refuses.
- **How found:** tangency-labs/solidcore#3214, tracing the failing edge in the Rust port, which kept the C++ sequence.
- **Confirmed:** yes, from the pinned source. The three trims share `sCurveDomain` and the proto edge takes its last value, as above. No C++ build was run, so the C++ outcome on the case's operands is not known: its intersection may place the crossing differently.

The Rust fix in `topology/proto_topology/p2.rs` trims the 3D curve and its UV curves to the one interval none of their trims snaps (`exact_pieces::common_trim_interval`, then `trim_unsnapped` for each), as the offset has done since tangency-labs/solidcore#3013. The regression is the corpus case `booleans/13462-rod-cut-by-two-counter-wound-channel-coils` and `crates/solidcore-ops/tests/boolean.rs::a_rod_cut_by_two_counter_wound_channel_sweeps_is_valid`.
