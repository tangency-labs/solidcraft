Updates: WR-305

#### WR-305 (update, 2026-11-16). `classify_edge_interval` is removed; curved pieces along an edge are certified

`CurveClassification::classify_edge_interval` (`intersect/curve_class/p3.rs`) now classifies a curved interval on a face edge by default, and only by a certificate (`curve_piece_runs_along_edge`). The fork's midpoint sample is gone; straight pairs keep the end-point certificate of the 2026-11-13 update. Removed: the field `UpstreamFixes::classify_edge_interval`, its doc file and every test setting of it.

**The certificate.** Both ends of the interval drop onto the edge, and the curve over the interval and the edge's curve between the drops are proved within the intersection tolerance (the band) of each other as wholes, on enclosures (`curve_runs_along`, strict `<`). The drops only choose the edge piece: the gaps at its ends are part of the bound, so a poor drop can only refuse. No tangency is required of a piece whose ends the edge intersections classified; a piece longer than the band that lies within it runs along the edge.

- **Ends apart.** A piece whose two ends are within the band of each other is one point as far as the tolerance tells. It runs along an edge only all the way round a closed edge, proved against the edge's whole curve; otherwise it is left as the edge intersections left it. This keeps out the short sphere/box-corner arc of goldens `tess0033` (a Level 2 clean union on main), which lies within the band only because it is about as short as the band. `tess0033` is unchanged.
- **A closed edge.** On an edge whose two ends are one vertex, a piece through the vertex drops on either side of the parameter seam. Where the piece between the drops is not proved, an end dropping exactly onto one end of the edge's interval is taken at its other end; else the curve is cut at the drop of the seam's point and each part is proved against its edge piece (`runs_through_seam`).
- **Iso-line pieces.** A curve-on-surface piece whose UV curve is a line along a parameter direction is enclosed by the degree-1 segment between its evaluated end UVs (the other coordinate is exact, the one along the line monotone), so it is bounded like a B-spline trim (`piece_tube`).
- **Unclassified ends.** An end the edge intersections left unclassified still needs the curve tangent to the edge at both ends of the interval (`tangent_to_edge_at_ends`, read from inside the piece) and the whole piece within the band (`piece_within_band`).

Every way the certificate declines (a failed drop, two drops at one parameter, ends within the band on an open edge, a bound not proved, an edge with no 3D curve) leaves the interval as the edge intersections left it, which is main's result. An error from evaluating the curves or dropping a point onto the edge propagates as the classification's error.

**The edge tolerance raise (#18065).** A curved piece not proved within the band can still lie on the edge to within a vertex's tolerance: a round's arc leaving a straight edge tangentially at its end departs quadratically, so it stays in the band over most of its length and leaves it inside the ball its end vertex already has. For an open edge, with the piece's ends farther than the band apart and dropping at two parameters, `tail_gap_within_vertex_ball` takes `ball`, the largest tolerance of an edge vertex whose ball holds an end of the piece, and needs it wider than the band. It then bounds the distance between the piece and the edge piece between the drops as wholes (`run_along_gap_bound`: the same pairs and halving as `curve_runs_along`, widest bound first, at most `RUN_ALONG_PIECES` cuts; every pair's bound holds whatever the cuts, so the widest bounds the whole). A bound `G` below the band classifies the piece directly. A bound `G <= ball` is returned as an `EdgeToleranceRaise`: the caller (`query/face/p2.rs`) raises the edge's tolerance to `G` (`topology::edge::set_tolerance`, only if larger, cascading to the vertices) and only then classifies the interval on the edge (`CurveClassification::set_edge_interval`). `G` is a certified upper bound of the real gap over the whole piece, the edge is never raised past a tolerance its own end already has, and every later check reads the edge's tolerance live, so it sees the raise. A bound past `ball` leaves the piece alone. The bound covers the whole piece, so no tangency or position of the gap is required: any piece within `G` of the edge lies within the raised edge's tolerance (the arc leaving tangentially is the case that reaches it). The cascade can raise the edge's other vertex to `G` as well. Every candidate edge is tried first: an edge the piece runs along as it is wins over a raise. Every caller of `CurveOnClassify` (booleans, queries, sweep, offset, face splits) can raise an edge this way, on the B-rep it classifies on. The raise lands while the intervals are classified in order: the edge crossings found before it stay valid (the raise only widens), and later intervals on that edge see the raised tolerance. Follow-up: `run_along_gap_bound` spends its whole cut budget when the true gap is past the band; an early stop once the bound is within `ball` and no longer shrinking would only save work.
