Updates: WR-647

#### WR-647 (addendum 2026-11-17): contacts seen through the tolerance are decided on merged stretches; `simple_coincidence_crossing_is_point` is retired

- **Status in the port:** `UpstreamFixes::simple_coincidence_crossing_is_point` is gone, with its doc, the preset line in `crates/smlib/src/core.rs`, its `fixes.json` entry and the flag settings in the integration tests. `Curve::simple_coincidence_checker` (`curve/curve/p6.rs`) returns upstream's coincidence range again in every case. The flag's per-span-pair rules (c2e3d9d's crossing case and 04b42b4's case 3e) are not kept.
- **Why the flag could not be retired as it was:** one Bezier span pair cannot tell a grazing crossing from tolerance-scale fit noise. With the flag on, the #1036 coincident skins (a section curve that is a fitted copy of the edge, 2.6e-6 to 1.7e-5 off at tolerance 2e-5, changing sides at random) came apart into points, and the Booleans gave wrong bodies (A - B empty, A ∩ B all of A, A ∪ B failing).
- **The rule now:** `Curve::contacts_seen_through_tolerance` (`curve/curve/p8.rs`) runs after the ranges are merged: in `GlobalCurveIntersect` before `classify_ranges` and at the single-leaf exit, and in `GlobalCurveSolve` on the parallel projected path. Each stretch (ranges that overlap or meet) is judged as a whole:
  - **Crossing:** the separations at the stretch's two ends point to opposite sides and the curves part at both ends (each end lies inside both curves). The answer is the one crossing `local_curve_intersect` finds strictly inside the stretch, nearer than every range end (#1979's ring lip), and only when `curves_meet_at_most_once` certifies over the whole stretch that the curves meet at most once: the cones of their first derivatives (from the Bezier legs, rounding counted) part, or, where the curves are close to parallel, a subdivision of both stretches drops every pair of pieces whose hulls are apart by more than twice the meeting zero (the touch's evaluated points, and rounding, counted; so two meetings within the zero of each other are never split into separate pairs) and the cones over the pairs left part, or one curve is an ellipse (or a parallel projection of one) whose quadratic, taken along the other curve's pieces, is strictly monotone. Without the certificate the stretch keeps upstream's ranges (a stretch the curves cross three times stays ranges).
  - **Touch:** the stretch is all the solutions and the curves part at one or both ends. The touch is the least `Minimize` result started from every range end. It is taken for one point only when (a) it is nearer than where the curves part, (b) the curves **meet** there: the evaluated distance is at most `SM_EFF_ZERO * (1 + max coordinate)`, after a `SignedDirectedMinimize` refinement across the tangent when `Minimize` stalls short of a flat tangency, (c) it is not on a fold of a curve against itself, and (d) `touch_is_alone` certifies over the whole stretch that it is the only meeting: one curve is straight (one degree-1 span), the other's signed distance `g` from the line is a B-spline whose second-derivative coefficients are all at least `κ > 0` (Bernstein bound over the stretch, rounding counted per coefficient), and the least value of `g` over the stretch allowed by `κ`, `g(t0) - (|g'(t0)| + e)² / (2κ)`, is at least minus scaled zero, or (a non-rational curve) the parameter width where `g` can be within scaled zero, times the curve's speed bound, is within scaled zero; or `curves_meet_at_most_once` holds (curves that pass through each other in space, as a line through a Hermite curve); or `meetings_lie_in_one_interval` holds. That needs one curve to be a planar conic (an ellipse, its control points within scaled zero of a plane through them), and it checks two bands, each one interval (one run of pieces inside the band, monotone pieces — Bernstein differences of one sign — before and after that run, and pieces past the band beyond them, with the stretch halved at most 24 times into at most 4096 pieces and every rounding counted). (i) The other curve's homogeneous offset from the plane, `g = n . (x w - w p0)`, is within the band `(|n| z + eps_b) w_max` (`z` the scaled zero, `eps_b` the plane bound) on such a run. (ii) Over the core of that run, the conic's quadratic `w² G = l1² + l2² - l3²`, taken along the other curve, is within the quadric band `(eps_g + a z (2 sqrt(1 + eps_g) + a z)) w_max²` on one run. Here `eps_g` bounds `|G|` on the conic's own stretch and `a` bounds the gradient of `G`'s linear forms. Every meeting lies in both bands, so all meetings lie in one interval of the other curve's parameter, over which that curve stays within both bands. The bands are scaled by `w_max` and `w_max²`, so where the other curve's weight is lower they are wider in distance, by that ratio. The touch takes this certificate only when that interval is shorter than a tenth of the shorter curve (`approximate_length`, `classify_ranges`' own large-interval rule). An exact overlap with the conic over an arc is certified to lie in one interval too, but that interval is long, so it keeps its range (`wr_647_an_exact_overlap_with_a_conic_stays_a_range`). A planar spline (no conic quadratic) gets no band certificate. This certifies a section of a surface tangent to a floor plane along the floor's edge arc (a fourth-order touch, within 1e-10 over 0.027 of the stretch: `solidcore-ops` `boolean::an_insert_whose_top_is_tangent_to_the_floor_at_a_corner_round_joins_its_bin`). Curves in one plane (every piece within the band) get no band certificate. Two curved curves without one of these certificates, or a curve with a corner on the stretch, keep upstream's range (an exact overlap, `κ = 0`, stays a range).
  - Everything else keeps upstream's ranges: a coincidence running to a curve end (#1036, concentric arcs, an edge against its fitted copy), curves within tolerance that never meet, several touches, and crossings the solver does not find.
- **Never-meeting curves are ranges (the reviewer's probe):** a curve within 4e-6 of a line over x in [-0.875, 0.875] at tolerance 1e-5, leaving on the same side, is a coincidence at that tolerance, not a touch. Upstream returns the range; so does the port. Test: `wr_647_a_curve_lying_within_tolerance_on_part_of_a_line_stays_a_range`.
- **Flat touches are points (the defined convention):** a curve within tolerance of a line over a long stretch that meets it exactly once (a flat touch, 4e-6 high at tolerance 1e-5 elsewhere) is one point, while the same curve moved 4e-6 off, never meeting the line, is a range. The meeting is what decides: one certified meeting inside a stretch is the contact, and a stretch with no meeting stays upstream's coincidence range. Tests: `wr_647_a_flat_curve_touching_a_line_meets_it_at_one_point` and the never-meeting probe above.
- **Perspective and rotation projections keep their ranges:** their projected derivatives are upstream's secant approximations, and the solver stops short of the contact (goldens `curve.curve_p9` gen0088 found 8.6e-5 where the true contact is 7.9e-9).
- **Tests:** `crates/solidcore/tests/wr_647_concentric_arcs_nearest_point.rs` (crossing, touch, flat touch, never-meeting and exact-overlap cases, coincidence to a curve end, three crossings in one stretch kept as a range, two touches in one stretch kept as a range), `issue_1979_ring_lip_crossing_not_coincidence.rs` (default kernel, every offset valid), `issue_1036_coincident_skin_sides.rs` (no flag), `wr_647_two_meetings_within_a_planar_curves_band_are_both_kept` (two meetings 1/16 apart within one plane band both stay), the `curve/curve/p8.rs` unit tests of the band certificate (a curve leaving a circle's plane as the fourth power meets it in one cluster; two touches across the plane, two meetings apart within the plane, curves in one plane and a planar spline are not certified) and of the subdivision (two meetings within the meeting zero are not "at most once"), goldens `curve_curve_p8::tests::range_end_refinement_keeps_the_noncollapsed_tail` (its original name, inputs and intent, strengthened) and `moved_contacts_lie_in_their_base_ranges`, and `intersect_topology_solver_p1::tests::wr_647_gen2408_meets_inside_its_base_range`.

- **Known limits:** the touch is the least of local minima started from every range end, not a global minimisation. The band certificate puts all meetings in one parameter interval of the other curve. That interval can be an exact overlap: a curve lying on the conic over an arc and leaving its plane on both sides is certified, with the overlap as its interval. So the touch takes the certificate only when the interval's `approximate_length` is below a tenth of the shorter curve's. That is the length below which `classify_ranges` does not keep a run as a large interval. A longer run keeps upstream's range (review r4's 60 degree overlap: interval 1.15 long against a limit of 0.15). Between those two lengths, a curve that follows the conic within the zero over a run shorter than that limit is one touch: the `Minimize` point. The `solidcore-ops` tangent-floor case has a 0.037 run against a limit of 0.18. The isolation test uses the curvature at the touch only; a stretch whose curvature changes strongly between the touch and its ends is judged by the curvature at the touch. Straight pieces have no curvature, so a touch between them (goldens polyline_touching_vertex, two polylines touching at a vertex) is not certified isolated and keeps upstream's range.
