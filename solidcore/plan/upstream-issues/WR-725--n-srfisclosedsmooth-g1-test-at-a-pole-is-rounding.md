#### WR-725. `N_SrfIsClosedSmooth` decides G1 across a seam at a pole by the rounding of two vanishing derivatives
- **Severity:** Wrong result
- **Area:** NLib
- **Location:** `source/SMLib/src/NL_SrfNurb.cpp:5016`, `N_SrfIsClosedSmooth`: the G1 test of the u closure (`N_VectorsAngle(D1[1][0], D2[1][0])`, around `:5136`) and of the v closure (`D[0][1]`); `N_VectorsAngle` in `NL_Geom.cpp:1077`.
- **What happens:** For the u closure the function samples the two seam columns `u = U[0]` and `u = U[r]` at `q + 1` stations per v span and takes the angle between the two first derivatives across the seam, `S_u`. Where a boundary row of the net is collapsed to one point (a sphere's or a surface of revolution's pole on the seam's end), `S_u` is zero in real arithmetic, and what the evaluation returns is rounding: exact zeros, or vectors of about 1e-15 in any direction. `N_VectorsAngle` then fails with `NL_GEO_ERR` (two zeros) or returns the angle between two noise vectors (anything from 0 to 180 degrees). So the surface is refused with error 17, reported not smooth, or (by chance) smooth, by the last bits of the derivative at the one station where the angle has no meaning. Of 4000 turned copies of each of the two spheres below, none was reported closed and smooth in u (5502 refused with 17, 2498 reported not smooth). `N_SrfEvalPtDerivsUnbounded` then extends such a surface past its seam instead of wrapping its parameter (and WR-166's stored-flag error follows from the `NL_GEO_ERR` outcome).
- **Trigger:** `nlib.srf_nurb` cases `gen0652` (tols `[0.001, 1]`) and `gen1542` (tols `[0.001, 0.5]`), `closed_smooth`, `n = 1`, u closure: rational biquadratic spheres (u knots `[0,0,0,.25,.25,.5,.5,.75,.75,1,1,1]`, v knots `[0,0,0,.5,.5,1,1,1]`) whose v = 0 and v = 1 columns are poles. `S_u` is exactly zero at the pole on both sides; C++ and Rust both return 17. The same `gen1542` sphere turned by -158.13 degrees about an axis leaves `S_u = (0, ±1.8e-15, 0)` on the two sides, 180 degrees apart: reported not smooth.
- **How found:** generated difftest case, while changing the order of operations of a rational surface's first derivatives (the outcome of these cases changed with it). Filed as tangency-labs/solidcore#2521.
- **Confirmed:** yes, cases `gen0652` and `gen1542` of `nlib.srf_nurb` (status 17 on both sides).
- **Rust port (opt-in):** `Kernel::fixes.closed_smooth_pole_tangent_limit` (`UpstreamFixes`, default `false`), read by `Surface::is_closed_smooth_with_fixes` / `Surface::is_closed_smooth_flag_with_fixes`. At a station where a side's derivative across the seam is not longer than `0.001 * NL_MTOL` (the pole test of `N_SrfEvalPtNormalAtPole`) that side's direction is the limit of `S_u` along the seam, the mixed derivative `S_uv` (`S_u(u, v) = (v - v0) S_uv + O((v - v0)^2)` at a collapsed row `v0`), negated at the end of the v domain, as `N_SrfEvalPtNormalAtPole` takes `S_uv x S_v` for the pole normal; the v closure likewise. Every other station is unchanged, bit for bit (its derivatives are evaluated with the mixed one, which does not change the lower orders). Where the mixed derivative vanishes too (a degenerate patch) the angle fails as before. Tests: `closed_smooth_pole_of_sphere_is_smooth`, `closed_smooth_pole_noise_does_not_decide` in `crates/solidcore/src/nurbs/srf_nurb.rs`. Not yet passed by `N_SrfEvalPtDerivsUnbounded`'s callers, which hold no `Kernel`. Solidcore-native, no fork commit.
