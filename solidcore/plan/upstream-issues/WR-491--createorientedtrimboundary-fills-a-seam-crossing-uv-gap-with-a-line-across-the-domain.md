#### WR-491. `CreateOrientedTrimBoundary` fills a UV gap of one period (a seam crossing) with a line across the whole domain, so the face integrates to nothing
- **Severity:** Wrong result
- **Area:** Topology / trimming, mass properties
- **Location:** `source/SMLib/src/SmFace.cpp`, `SmFace::CreateOrientedTrimBoundary` (def. `:7333` at 3fd5b95). The gap fill is `:7443-7455` for an edge use along its edge and `:7526-7539` for one against it ("If there is a gap create a line to fill it", `SmLine::CreateLineSegment` from the last curve's end to the next one's start). `SmFace::CreateUVTrimCurves` (`SmFace.cpp`) makes each edge use's UV curve on its own and closes only small UV gaps (`sm_FixUVTrimCurves`), so nothing brings a trim that landed a period away back beside its neighbour. The filled boundary is what `SmFace::ComputePreciseProperties` integrates.
- **What happens:** the fill is meant for surface singularities, where a gap is real in UV and zero in 3D. Where consecutive UV trims of a face on a closed (periodic) surface end and start on opposite sides of the seam, the gap is one period in U (or V) and again zero in 3D, but the line crosses the whole domain. The loop doubles back on itself and its Green-theorem integral cancels: the face's area is about 0 and its volume term is wrong, with no error status. In the insert below, 16 of the 313 faces are thread flanks (cones, one turn each) whose areas are 1.087 and 0.403 mm² when intact; they integrate to 1e-4 and below.
- **Trigger:** a face on a cone (any closed surface) whose edge curves end a few tens of micrometres off their vertices, so that a seam edge's UV trim lies on one side of the seam and the rail's end on the other. A frustum (base radius 3, top radius 2, height 4) whose seam edge is turned about the axis by an arc of 5e-5 at the base shows it: `crates/solidcore/tests/trim_boundary_seam_wrap.rs`. Offsets from 1e-5 to 5e-3 all collapse the lateral face; 5e-6 and 0 do not (the trim snaps to the seam).
- **How found:** a threaded M3 insert imported from STEP through SolidCraft's solidcore kernel: exact area 176.22 mm² against 187.65 (truck and a mesh), volume 39.73 (35.50 after a zero-length move) against 39.71. The body's native data was dumped as `SmBrepData` and rebuilt on both kernels.
- **Confirmed:** yes, our own C++ run (v1.0.1 at 3fd5b95, `SmBrep::ReadFromFile` of the dumped data, `SmFace::ComputePreciseProperties` per face and `SmApiBrepComputeVolume`, accuracy 1e-6, 3 runs, no differences):

  | body | C++ volume / area | Rust volume / area (default) |
  |------|-------------------|------------------------------|
  | the insert as imported | 39.729626 / 176.222946 | 39.729626 / 176.222925 |
  | the insert after a zero move | 35.502980 / 168.361215 | 35.502980 / 168.361217 |

  Every per-face area agrees to 1e-7 (the intact flank 1.087107135 in both, a collapsed one 7.1e-5 in both).
- **Fix:** where the gap is whole periods in a direction in which the surface is closed (to twice the face tolerance), to within 1% of a period, shift the curves after it by the period instead of adding the line; a loop whose shifts do not cancel keeps upstream's lines. With it the insert gives volume 39.729626 and area 187.459 as imported, 187.351 after the zero move (the rest of the shortfall to 187.65 is not traced; face 178 of the moved body is only partly restored because its cone is 7.8e-4 from closed).
- **Rust port:** faithful (the default is the C++ behaviour above). Opt-in `Kernel::fixes.trim_boundary_seam_wrap`, `crates/solidcore/src/topology/face/p1.rs` (`create_oriented_trim_boundary`, `seam_wrap`, `seam_periods`, `shift_curve`).
