Updates: WR-57

#### WR-57 (update, 2026-11-16). `degen_param_tiebreak_uses_actual_param` is removed; the guesses are parameters, the input's first

The fix is the default and its flag is gone (tangency-labs/solidcraft#14246). Turned on alone, the flag's mapped tie-break (`s_ivl.evaluate(f)`) turned goldens `surface.surface_p7` gen0435 from converged into `SM_ERR`: it picked sample 0, and upstream starts the Newton solve at `sSIvl.Evaluate(f)`, which for `f = 0` is the domain boundary, there a collapsed corner (`|S_v|` 3.3e-11) where the tangent across the singular boundary is undefined. Upstream measures each guess at the pulled-in sample `sample_domain.evaluate(f)` (`SmSurface.cpp:6873`-`:6900`) but compares, starts and falls back at a different point.

`Surface::find_degen_param_for_direction` (`surface/surface/p7.rs`) now works with the parameters where the alignment is measured: the near-equal tie-break compares them with `dS`, both Newton solves start at them, and an unconverged answer is the guess itself. The input parameter `dS` is the first near-best guess when it lies in the interval and its tangent is defined, since of nearly equal guesses the one nearest the input is kept and the input is nearest itself. That part is needed: where the alignment is the same over a stretch of parameters, the Newton solve stops at its start. With only the samples as guesses, a pole inversion there returns a sample rather than the input, even when the input is already an answer. Goldens `topology.brep_mirror` mat0076 then hung: each inversion of its drop curve jumped 0.86 in `v`, so every span failed and was subdivided down to the 1/8000 floor. The solve finds where the alignment is stationary, or a kink in it, so a new start can end at a minimum or past a kink downhill: from the samples, goldens `surface.surface_p7` gen2112 moved from a maximum (alignment 0.463) to a minimum (0.335), and gen0415 converged to an alignment of -0.29. An answer now counts as converged only where it is a local maximum of the alignment on the closed interval, checked with the solve's own `F = A'` and `F' = A''`: an end where the alignment grows outward, a stationary point (`|F| < tol`) with `F' <= SM_EFF_ZERO`, polished by one Newton step, or a knot where the one-sided slopes change from `+` to `-` (a kink, where the solve's bisection stops beside the knot). Each sign is charged its rounding, bounded from the certified partials of the surface's net (`alignment_slopes`; a curve-bounded surface evaluates through its basis, so its basis's net is charged, looking through at most 16 nested bases): an end's and a kink's slopes must be proven, and a stationary point's curvature counts as not positive unless it is proven above `SM_EFF_ZERO`, so a plateau keeps its input. A kind with no certified evaluator proves no end and no kink (their charges are infinite) and keeps upstream's float test only for a stationary point; a stationary point is accepted only where its curvature charge is finite. Where the net has a certified evaluator but a charge has no bound (near a collapsed corner, where the derivative across the singular direction is not above its error), the charge is infinite and proves no sign. At an end where the net's row of control points is exactly one point (a pole, its knots clamped) the end is a maximum where the certified limit of `F` from inside grows outward (`collapsed_end_slope`: `w = (s - e) g(s)`, so the tangent tends to `±w_s / |w_s|` and `F` to `±(P w_ss / 2) / |w_s| . dir`), the alignment taken at its limit there (goldens `surface_p7` gen0548, gen0952). Where the derivative is not enclosed away from zero but the row is a pole only to the rounding (rational rows whose `x_j / w_j` differ in the last bits: gen0169's curve-bounded basis, gen2767), the solve's stationary answer is accepted as upstream accepts it; the tangent and the alignment are not defined to the rounding there, and main's answers are kept. Where the surface is closed in the singular direction (`IsClosed` over the natural domain) the ends meet at the seam, so an end where the alignment grows outward is a maximum only when it also grows toward the seam at the other end (a crease); otherwise the solve restarts at the other end and its answer is checked like any other. Without this, `wr_550_projection_through_sphere_poles` failed: its sphere's maximum lies 0.00176 past the seam, the end u = 0 was accepted, and the pole trace took its derivatives off the curve's meridian. The answer is the most aligned of the maxima the solve reaches from the near-best guess and from the best sample and of the interval's ends where they are maxima, the first on a tie within `SM_EFF_ZERO`, so an input on a plateau stays. Of nearly equal guesses upstream keeps the one nearest the input (`fTol`), but two maxima are not two guesses: goldens `surface.surface_p7` gen0415 has a maximum at each end (0.1461 at the input's end, 0.1611 at the other) and the larger is kept. A maximum less aligned than the best sample is not the largest one: then the solve also starts at every sample and at upstream's ten starts, and then runs on each bracket between neighbouring ends, starts, samples and knots whose one-sided slopes are `+` then `-`, for a peak narrower than the starts' spacing (gen0971's peak at -1.4389, `A''` about -563, which main misses and reports the end -1.9062 found). Where no maximum at least as aligned as the best sample is found, the answer is unconverged: the most aligned of the best maximum found, the best sample and upstream's return. Upstream accepts any answer the solve reports found. `fTol` stays only in upstream's near-equal sample tie-break. Removed: the field `UpstreamFixes::degen_param_tiebreak_uses_actual_param`, SolidCraft's preset line, its entries in `fixes.json` and `issue_2260`'s list, and the flag test.


The correct maximum exposed a defect in its one consumer, `Surface::invert_point_near_singularity` (`surface/surface/p8.rs`, upstream `InvertPointNearSingularity`). That method assumes the boundary at the guess is a collapsed pole and drops the test point onto the iso curve at the degenerate parameter without checking the result. Where the boundary is not collapsed, that iso curve can lie far from the test point: in goldens `surface_p8` gen1191 the boundary `u = 1` runs from 4.0 to 36085 away from the point, the search converges at the end `v = -0.652`, and the drop lands 35505.9 away, while the guess is 1395.2 away. Upstream reports that as found, and the point-solve stepper ends its iteration there. Main returned `SM_ERR` (1001) only because the old search stopped elsewhere. The method now refuses an answer whose certified distance from the test point is provably greater than the guess's (`SM_ERR`), each distance enclosed with the certified distance's own rounding and the evaluation's (`point_rounding`: `gamma(8 (p + q + 2))` times the net's largest coordinate, grown past the knots by the end spans' extrapolation, plus the weight term of a rational net). A distance without an enclosure (not a number, or past a rational net's knots) is refused, never compared. The guess's own point, evaluated again as the answer, therefore never counts as farther than itself (`invert_near_singularity_keeps_the_guess_own_point_on_a_far_net`: a net 3e9 from the origin, where an allowance in ulps of the distance refused it). A margin in the distance comparison instead (of `tol`, or of 16 ulps) let an answer tie its guess and end the stepper early: goldens `surface_p4` thin0064 then ended 2.7e-7 farther than main at `tol` 1e-9. The stepper then takes its normal Newton step from the guess. This change is unconditional: no answer proven farther from the point than the guess is the closest point.

The drop's iso curve is also not always the nearest one: the most aligned iso curve passes through the closest point only where the boundary is a collapsed pole. Where it is not collapsed, or the alignment is flat along it, a less aligned iso curve can pass nearer (goldens `surface_p8` gen1651, gen1460, and gen1191, whose drop at the search's answer the refusal above rejects). The search now also returns the parameters where its Newton solve reports found from each of upstream's ten starts, every answer upstream's search can give converged; the method drops onto the search's answer and onto each of those, and keeps the nearest drop. A later drop replaces the kept one only where it is proven nearer: the upper end of its certified distance below the lower end of the kept one's, so drops equally near within the distances' rounding keep the search's answer. The refusal applies to the drop kept. The drop also assumes the boundary is a pole: every point of it is the same point. Where it is not, a point of the boundary itself can be nearer than the drop: the method drops the test point onto the boundary's own iso curve, starting at the answer's parameter, and refuses (`SM_ERR`) where that boundary point's certified distance is below the drop's (goldens `surface_p8` gen1191, whose boundary `u = 1` passes 9.4 from the point while the nearest drop is 1163.2 away, and gen0270).

The point-solve stepper also inverts near a singularity when it maximizes the distance (`SolverOperation::Maximize`), and ends its iteration at the answer. There the nearest drop is the wrong one: goldens `surface_point_solve_p3` matrix_0105 ended at a drop 18192 from the point against the guess's 21900, and review106's wrapped-sphere maximize rows at about 0. For a maximize the method (`invert_point_near_singularity_for`, `farthest`) keeps the drop proven farther, and where that drop is proven nearer than the guess the guess is the answer: stepping on from the guess instead ends nearer still (matrix_0822 0.591 and matrix_0957 0.619, against main's 0.592 and 1.163). Neither the guess nor a drop is shown to be a maximum (the drops minimize along their iso curves), so a maximize's inversion is reported unable to converge either way: matrix_0105 keeps main's point and main's `UnableToConverge`.
