#### WR-711. A round's exact circular sections change arc count where they sweep across 91 degrees, and the skin between them leaves the circles (1.97e-3 off a variable round)
- **Severity:** Wrong result (accuracy)
- **Area:** Fillets (sections)
- **Location:** `source/SMLib/src/SmFilletStandardSolver.cpp`, `SmCircularCrossSectionFSG::CreateSurfaceFromCurves` with `m_bApproximateArcs` FALSE (each section is `SmBSplineCurve::CreateCircleSegment(..., SM_CO_QUADRATIC)`, one pass), and `N_CreateQuadraticArcTol` (`source/SMLib/src/NL_CrvCommon.cpp`), which takes one rational quadratic arc for a sweep under 91 degrees and two above. The fork's `SmGnSectionFSG::CircleSurfaceFromCurves` (`source/SMLib/src/SmFilletSection.cpp`) has the same loop and call. A fix of WR-708, which turns the exact circles on for the executive's rounds.
- **What happens:** the approximated arcs take two passes: the first finds the most knots among the sections, and the second builds every section again with that many. The exact circles take one pass, so a round whose sections sweep across 91 degrees along the rails has one-arc sections (weights `1, cos(a/2), 1`) beside two-arc sections (weights `1, cos(a/4), 1, cos(a/4), 1`). `CreateSkinnedSurface` makes them compatible by inserting the knot 0.5 into the one-arc sections. That gives a rational parameterisation unlike the two-arc sections': the middle weight is `(1 + cos(a/2))/2`, about 0.854, where its neighbours have 1. The skin interpolates the homogeneous control points through all sections, so its weights swing between the stations (0.82 to 1.12 along the middle row), and between the stations the surface leaves the circles. The rails' point sections, where they meet, are degree 1 point curves of weight 1. Beside circles of weights `cos(a/4)` they do the same at the round's point end.
- **Trigger:** the box 10 x 20 x 30, its edge x = 10, z = 30 rounded by the progressive cubic radius law `[2, 2, 6, 3, 0]` (Greville cubic over [0, 1], `SmApiVariableRadiusFillet` with the circular section; the fork's capi test `section_variable_round_spline_holds`). The sections sweep exactly 90 degrees where the law turns (r' = 0: one arc) and a little more elsewhere (two arcs). At 5e8c9c1, the round is 1.966136e-3 off the union of its balls (17 x 17 points over its domain; worst mid-section, y about 2.95), against 9.99e-5 for the approximated arcs at 58d03ef, the parent of 523bc25. Its volume is 5957.716621492882, 1.88e-3 under the balls' envelope's 5957.718502.
- **How found:** `fork_ea59e9c_section_orders::the_progressive_cubic_variable_round_lies_on_its_balls_union_and_is_g1` (#2300) failed with every fix on. The port instrumented at each section printed the arc count and weights (one-arc sections at the law's turns, t = 17.5 to 18.8 and 28.6 to 30.1, the rest two-arc). The fork's 5e8c9c1 driver (SmFilletExecutive as `SmApiVariableRadiusFillet` runs it) printed its round's net: 60 x 5, V knots `0 (3), 0.5 (2), 1 (3)`, and the same swinging middle weights (0.8272, 0.8545, 0.8526, 0.8235, 1.0658, ...) as the port's. Rebuilding the one-arc sections as two arcs took the round to 3.4e-4 off. Giving the point end the circles' weights as well took it to 8.1e-5 off.
- **Confirmed:** yes, on the fork's 5e8c9c1 build (and 523bc25, the same value): 1.966136e-3 off, the same as the port without the fix. The pinned C++ never builds exact circles for the executive's rounds (WR-708); its exact-arc generator is `SmFilletCorner`'s, with the same single pass.
- **Rust port:** opt-in `Kernel::fixes.fillet_exact_arc_spans`, off by default (a fix of WR-708's `fillet_exact_arc_sections`). With exact circles, the first pass notes the most and fewest arcs among the sections. If they differ, a second pass builds each circle of fewer arcs again as that many equal arcs (`BSplineCurve::create_circle_segment_min_arcs`, `quadratic_arc_min_arcs`), tweaked onto the rails and mirrored as on the first pass. This is the approximated arcs' own second pass. Each point section then becomes the circle nearest it along the rails, collapsed onto its point, with the same arcs and weights. The progressive cubic round is then 8.15e-5 off its balls' union (the approximated arcs' 1.0e-4; 8.7e-5 on a 200 x 32 grid) and its volume is 5957.718678529 (1.8e-4 over the envelope, 3.0e-8 relative; the approximated arcs are 6.6e-4 under). The linear round on its cone stays within 2.3e-10 of the cone. With the flag off, the values are the fork's 5e8c9c1 values.
- **Tests:** `crates/solidcore/tests/fork_ea59e9c_section_orders.rs` (`wr711_exact_circles_of_one_arc_count_keep_the_progressive_round_on_its_balls`, the progressive cubic case against its envelope's volume `HOLDS_VOLUME`); `crates/solidcore/tests/wr685_variable_round_touch_circle.rs` (the fork's 5e8c9c1 values with the flag off, and the cone and cliff rounds with it on).
