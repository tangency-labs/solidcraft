#### WR-779. `SmCPGlobalSolver::LocalSolve` keeps points that are no minimum (maximum) of the distance, and `SM_SR_ALL` with a wide tolerance returns them
- **Severity:** Wrong result
- **Area:** Geometry queries
- **Location:** `source/SMLib/src/SmCurve.cpp:11391` to `:11455` (the boundary point test) and `:11457` (the answer is saved), in `SmCPGlobalSolver::LocalSolve` (def. `:10951`).
- **What happens:** Each guess of a span is solved by a Newton search for a parameter where the distance is stationary, and the point the search stops at is saved as a solution. Two kinds of saved point are not the extremum that was asked for.
  1. A stationary point of the other kind. The equation of `SM_SO_MINIMIZE` and `SM_SO_MAXIMIZE` is the same, so a maximum search that starts near a minimum stops at the minimum (and the reverse). Nothing tests which kind the point is.
  2. The end of a span next to a root. `LocalPropertyAnalysis` accepts a point as a root of the projected equation when the equation is below `SM_EFF_ZERO_SQRT`. A span that ends that close to a root reports its own end. The test at `:11391` drops a span end when the answer lies in the next span, but it measures its angle only for `SM_SO_MINIMIZE`, `SM_SO_MAXIMIZE`, `SM_SO_INTERSECT` and `SM_SO_RAYFIRE`. For `SM_SO_PROJECTED_MINIMIZE` and `SM_SO_PROJECTED_MAXIMIZE` the angle stays zero and the end is kept.

  With `SM_SR_SINGLE` the best solution hides both. With `SM_SR_ALL` every solution within the tolerance of the best is returned, so a wide tolerance returns them as if they were minima (maxima).
- **Trigger:** the straight degree-one spline with control points (-1/1024, 1, 0), (0, 1, 0), (1/1024, 1, 0) and knots [0, 0, 0.5, 2, 2], the point at the origin, interval [0, 2], `SM_SR_ALL`, tolerance 1.0. The distance is smallest at t = 0.5 (value 1) and largest at the two ends (value 1.0000004768370445). Measured on the port before the change, which followed the C++ line for line here:
  - `SM_SO_MAXIMIZE` returns the two ends and also t = 0.5, the minimum. On the projected wrapper of the curve it also returns t = 0.5002991210977101, where the distance is rising.
  - `SM_SO_PROJECTED_MINIMIZE` along Z on the projected wrapper returns t = 0.5 and also t = 0.5003662109375, 0.500732421875 and 0.50146484375: the starts of three spans next to the root, where the distance is rising.
- **How found:** tracing tangency-labs/solidcraft#13677 on the port, which followed the C++.
- **Confirmed:** by reading the C++ (the two places above) and on the port. A dense-sampling check of every point/curve minimum and maximum solve in the goldens (9.2 million solves, 2001 samples each) counts the returned points that a neighbour 1e-6 to 1e-3 of the interval away beats. `SM_SO_MAXIMIZE` with `SM_SR_ALL`: 45 such points without a target distance and 184 with one. Solidcore now tests the bend of the distance at each point a search stops at (`curve::curve::p1`, `distance_bend`), and gives a span end of a projected search to the span the distance improves into: 34 and 40 remain, and no solve loses a true answer. Tests `all_with_a_wide_tolerance_keeps_only_minima_or_only_maxima` and `a_corner_stays_the_farthest_or_nearest_point_of_a_polyline`.
- **Not covered:** the points that remain are span ends of the plain searches where the distance is still changing by less than the angular zero, or changing away from the next span. A stricter test (the distance must be stationary at every point inside the curve) removed nearly all of them, but it also dropped true answers in about 15 solves, so it is not part of the change.
