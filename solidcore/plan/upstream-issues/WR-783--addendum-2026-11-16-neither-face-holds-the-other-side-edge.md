#### WR-783 (update, 2026-11-16). Where neither face's surface holds the other side edge, the pick still went by the order of the side edges

The fix of 2026-11 picks the end face whose surface holds the whole other side edge. Where neither surface does, it kept upstream's first side, and the same open body came back on one side of a part and not on its mirror image.

- **Trigger:** tangency-labs/solidcraft#17617: a plate with a wall standing on it whose end face slants out past the plate's end. A round of 2 on one of the wall's two edges with the plate fails as "not a closed solid"; its mirror on the wall's other side builds. At the round's end V four edges meet: the rounded edge, the plate's corner edge, the slanted face's edge with the wall's side, and the edge between the plate's end and the slanted face. The plate's corner edge runs on past the slanted face's bounded surface, and the wall's edge leaves the plate's end plane, so neither surface holds the whole other side edge. Where the plate's end comes first round V, the cap is laid on its plane although the end rib runs from the plate's corner edge to the slanted face's edge, off that plane; the cap is left out and three edges stay free.
- **Fix in solidcore:** where neither surface holds the whole other side edge, the end face is the one whose tangent plane at V the other side edge leaves in, within the corner's tangency tolerance (`edge_leaves_in_face`): the cap's edge along that side edge starts at V, so it can lie on no other face's surface. Where both or neither do, the first side's face is kept, as upstream. The whole-edge test stays first: a test on the edge's piece next to V would need the end face's extended surface (the piece lies past the slanted face's bounded surface too), and the tangent test alone cannot decide #15768's End, whose two faces meet tangentially at V.
- **Tests:** `solidcore-ops` `tests/blend.rs` (`a_round_ending_where_a_slanted_wall_overhangs_its_plate_ends_on_the_wall`: both of the wall's edges, 12 faces, 28 edges, 18 vertices, the volume's closed form).
