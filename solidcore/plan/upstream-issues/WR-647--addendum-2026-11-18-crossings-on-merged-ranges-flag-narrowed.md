Updates: WR-647

#### WR-647 (addendum 2026-11-18): crossings decided on merged ranges; flag narrowed to the nearest-point half

- **Status in the port:** `UpstreamFixes::simple_coincidence_crossing_is_point` keeps only 04b42b4's case 3e: with it, `Curve::simple_coincidence_checker` (`curve/curve/p6.rs`) returns a span pair whose separation keeps one side without vanishing (taken across the curve) and whose largest distance exceeds the scaled zero as the one point where the curves come nearest, as on main. Its crossing half (c2e3d9d: a span pair whose separation changes sides is the crossing inside it) is retired: such a span pair is upstream's range in every kernel, and the crossing is decided on the merged stretch below. The production preset (`crates/smlib/src/core.rs`) still sets the flag, and `Kernel::default` leaves it off.
- **Why the crossing half could not stay:** one Bezier span pair cannot tell a grazing crossing from tolerance-scale fit noise. With the flag on, the #1036 coincident skins (a section curve that is a fitted copy of the edge, 2.6e-6 to 1.7e-5 off at tolerance 2e-5, changing sides at random) came apart into points, and the Booleans gave wrong bodies (A - B empty, A ∩ B all of A, A ∪ B failing).
- **The rule now:** `Curve::contacts_seen_through_tolerance` (`curve/curve/p8.rs`) runs after the ranges are merged: in `GlobalCurveIntersect` before `classify_ranges` and at the single-leaf exit, and in `GlobalCurveSolve` on the parallel projected path. Each stretch (ranges that overlap or meet) is judged as a whole:
  - **Crossing:** the separations at the stretch's two ends point to opposite sides and the curves part at both ends (each end lies inside both curves). The answer is the one crossing `local_curve_intersect` finds strictly inside the stretch, nearer than every range end (#1979's ring lip), and only when `curves_meet_at_most_once` certifies over the whole stretch that the curves meet at most once: the cones of their first derivatives (from the Bezier legs, rounding counted) part, or, where the curves are close to parallel, a subdivision of both stretches drops every pair of pieces whose hulls are apart by more than twice the meeting zero (the touch's evaluated points, and rounding, counted; so two meetings within the zero of each other are never split into separate pairs) and the cones over the pairs left part, or one curve is an ellipse (or a parallel projection of one) whose quadratic, taken along the other curve's pieces, is strictly monotone. Without the certificate the stretch keeps upstream's ranges (a stretch the curves cross three times stays ranges).
  - **Touch:** the stretch is all the solutions and the curves part at one or both ends. The touch is the least `Minimize` result started from every range end. It is taken for one point only when (a) it is nearer than where the curves part, (b) the curves **meet** there: the evaluated distance is at most `SM_EFF_ZERO * (1 + max coordinate)`, after a `SignedDirectedMinimize` refinement across the tangent when `Minimize` stalls short of a flat tangency, (c) it is not on a fold of a curve against itself, and (d) `touch_is_alone` certifies over the whole stretch that it is the only meeting: one curve is straight (one degree-1 span), the other's signed distance `g` from the line is a B-spline whose second-derivative coefficients are all at least `κ > 0` (Bernstein bound over the stretch, rounding counted per coefficient), and the least value of `g` over the stretch allowed by `κ`, `g(t0) - (|g'(t0)| + e)² / (2κ)`, is at least `-eps` (`eps` the rounding-scale bound on `|g|` where the curves meet exactly, `line_offsets`), or (a non-rational curve) the parameter width where `g` can be within `eps`, times the curve's speed bound, is within scaled zero; and in both cases the run where the curves are within the scaled zero `z` of each other (where `g <= 2 w_max z + eps`), at most `2 sqrt(2 (2 w_max z + eps - least) / κ)` long by the same quadratic bound, times the curve's speed bound (`curve_speed_bound`: `(max |X'| + max |c| max |w'|) / min w` from the control points, rational curves included), is within the tolerance (review r7 M2 and r8 B1: curves offset by less than `z` that never meet exactly have no exact meetings, so the one-interval argument alone held vacuously for a near overlap at the zero; a cut at a fraction of the stretch still took a 0.2 long near overlap for a point; tests `a_run_within_the_zero_over_the_stretch_is_not_a_lone_touch` and `wr_647_a_run_within_the_zero_resolved_by_the_tolerance_stays_a_range`); or `curves_meet_at_most_once` holds (curves that pass through each other in space, as a line through a Hermite curve). Two curved curves without one of these certificates, or a curve with a corner on the stretch, keep upstream's range (an exact overlap, `κ = 0`, stays a range).
  - Everything else keeps upstream's ranges: a coincidence running to a curve end (#1036, concentric arcs, an edge against its fitted copy), curves within tolerance that never come within the zero of each other over a run the tolerance resolves, several touches, and crossings the solver does not find.
- **Never-meeting curves are ranges (the reviewer's probe):** a curve within 4e-6 of a line over x in [-0.875, 0.875] at tolerance 1e-5, leaving on the same side, is a coincidence at that tolerance, not a touch. Upstream returns the range; so does the port. Test: `wr_647_a_curve_lying_within_tolerance_on_part_of_a_line_stays_a_range`.
- **When a touch is a point (the convention):** a touch of a line is one point only when the bound above puts the run where the curves are within the scaled zero `z` of each other within the tolerance (or `curves_meet_at_most_once` holds); otherwise the stretch keeps upstream's range, whether the curves meet exactly once or never. The bound is sufficient, not necessary: it is conservative by about 2x, so a run of half the tolerance can still be refused and keep its range. Guard rows near the cut (review r9: runs 4 and 1.015 times the tolerance stay ranges, a run of a tenth of it is one point) are in `wr_647_a_run_within_the_zero_resolved_by_the_tolerance_stays_a_range`. At the zero a flat exact touch and a near overlap offset below `z` cannot be told apart, so they get the same answer: a parabola `y = c x²` touching a line is a point while `2 sqrt(2 z / κ)` (times its speed) is within the tolerance, and a flatter one is a range. The flat quadratic touching the axis at the origin (heights `2e-6 t²`, run about 2.8e-3 at tolerance 1e-5) is a range, as upstream gives; an earlier head of this change made it a point. Tests: `wr_647_a_flat_touch_whose_run_within_the_zero_the_tolerance_resolves_stays_a_range`, `wr_647_a_run_within_the_zero_resolved_by_the_tolerance_stays_a_range` (review r8's rows, exact touch included), `wr_647_a_curve_lying_within_tolerance_on_part_of_a_line_stays_a_range` and the parabola touch tests.
- **Perspective and rotation projections keep their ranges:** their projected derivatives are upstream's secant approximations, and the solver stops short of the contact (goldens `curve.curve_p9` gen0088 found 8.6e-5 where the true contact is 7.9e-9).
- **Tests:** `crates/solidcore/tests/wr_647_concentric_arcs_nearest_point.rs` (crossing, touch, flat touch, never-meeting and exact-overlap cases, coincidence to a curve end, three crossings in one stretch kept as a range, two touches in one stretch kept as a range), `issue_1979_ring_lip_crossing_not_coincidence.rs` (default kernel, every offset valid), `issue_1036_coincident_skin_sides.rs` (no flag), `wr_647_two_meetings_within_a_planar_curves_band_are_both_kept` (two meetings 1/16 apart within one plane band both stay), `wr_647_an_exact_overlap_with_a_conic_stays_a_range`, `wr_647_short_exact_overlaps_with_a_conic_stay_ranges`, `wr_647_near_overlaps_within_the_zero_stay_ranges` and `wr_647_sub_tenth_near_overlaps_with_a_conic_stay_ranges` (exact and near overlaps with a planar conic stay ranges, the last with the flag off and on), main's five flag tests (the nearest point with the flag), the `curve/curve/p8.rs` unit test of the subdivision (two meetings within the meeting zero are not "at most once"), goldens `curve_curve_p8::tests::range_end_refinement_keeps_the_noncollapsed_tail` (its original name, inputs and intent, strengthened) and `moved_contacts_lie_in_their_base_ranges`, and `intersect_topology_solver_p1::tests::wr_647_gen2408_meets_inside_its_base_range`.

- **The #527 case (`range_end_refinement_keeps_the_noncollapsed_tail`):** base's answer was a point at t 3.372 (gap 0.343) and two ranges within the tolerance meeting at t 6.1104. The test keeps its name, inputs and intent (the range-end check must not lose the tail) and now asserts the stronger fact: the first point unchanged, and the tail with its head replaced by their junction, the one transversal crossing (27.7 degrees) of the line and the quadratic, with the closed-form certificate that the distance from the line grows at least linearly (`m >= 2.9`, exact 2.9413) away from it over the whole base stretch, so the stretch holds no coincidence. The #527 range-end check also stays covered by `issue_527_refine_cc_range_end` in `curve/curve/p1.rs`.
- **Known limits:** the touch is the least of local minima started from every range end, not a global minimisation. The isolation test uses the curvature at the touch only; a stretch whose curvature changes strongly between the touch and its ends is judged by the curvature at the touch. Straight pieces have no curvature, so a touch between them (goldens polyline_touching_vertex, two polylines touching at a vertex) is not certified isolated and keeps upstream's range.
- **No band or zero-run certificate for a tangency of two curved curves:** a tangency has no sign change, so "the curves meet at T" cannot be certified at the exact scale; it is read at the scaled zero `z`, and there it is a short coincidence. Earlier versions of this change tried to certify a conic-against-curve touch from the bands of the conic's plane and quadratic, and each version was shown a near or exact overlap that it collapsed. The last form, a certified run of meetings below a tenth of both natural intervals plus a Rolle refusal of exact overlaps (upstream's `classify_intersecting_range_solution` cut), fails the probe below: a spline whose middle span is a `theta` degree arc of the unit circle about (0.5, 1, 0), radius `1 + dr` or lifted `dz`, its knots stretched to 0, L, L + 1, 2L + 1 (same geometry), at tolerance 1e-2, both orders. A row is kept when a range covers s in [L, L + 1].
