Updates: WR-591

#### WR-591 (update, 2026-10-31). The fix turns about one normal for the whole list, so loops touching from outside close apart in every order
- **What happens (with the 9209e76 fix alone):** `next_round_the_vertex` took the normal it turns about at a vertex from the first pair of rays there that are not parallel, in the order of the curve ends in the list. Its sign changed with that order, so where two loops touch at a vertex from outside each other, `OrderCurvesIntoLoops` gave the two loops or one loop through the vertex twice round both (a figure-eight), depending on how the list was rotated.
- **Trigger:** `OrderCurvesIntoLoops` (tolerance `1e-6`, `loops_go_on_round_the_vertex` on) of the unit squares (0, 0)-(1, 1) and (1, 1)-(2, 2), touching at (1, 1), as eight lines: both counterclockwise or both clockwise, either square's lines first, every rotation of the list (32 orders). 12 of them gave one loop of area 2 instead of two loops of area 1.
- **How found:** SolidCraft; fixed in the fork as tangency-labs/smlib@b0ee3f9 ("loops: where loops touch at a vertex, turn about one normal for the whole list").
- **Confirmed:** yes, in the port with the flag on. The pinned C++ has no round-the-vertex step, so this is a defect of the fork's first fix, not of upstream.

The same opt-in field, `Kernel::fixes.loops_go_on_round_the_vertex`, now also takes the fork's follow-up: before it walks, `order_curves_into_loops` (`crates/solidcore/src/topology/trimming_tools.rs`) computes one turn normal (`loops_turn_normal`): each curve's samples `p_k` at quarters of its natural interval, about the curves' bounding-sphere centre `c`, add `(p_k - c) x (p_k+1 - c)`; the terms each taken the way of the largest give the plane, and the normal is turned against the loops' summed area when that is not negligible. `next_round_the_vertex` turns counterclockwise about that normal (and keeps the found end when it is zero) instead of a normal from the rays at the vertex. Default off: the pinned result is unchanged. `crates/solidcore/tests/wr_591_loops_touching_at_a_vertex.rs` (`wr_591_loops_touching_from_outside_close_apart`) checks the 32 orders give two loops of area 1 each; the 24 orders of the original record still close soundly.
