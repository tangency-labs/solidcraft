#### WR-743. `sm_TestForIsoCircles` takes the planes of two iso circles for intersecting when their normals differ by rounding, and the common-axis test refuses a small torus far from the origin
- **Severity:** Wrong result (a NURBS torus is not recognised as its analytic surface, so SolidCraft reports `a NURBS form that is not its analytic surface`)
- **Area:** Surfaces / surface of revolution
- **Location:** `source/SMLib/src/SmSurfOfRevolution.cpp`, `sm_TestForIsoCircles` (the common-axis check between iso circles), calling `smgu_IntersectTwoPlanes` (`source/SMLib/src/SmGeomUtility.cpp:3235`).
- **What happens:** the normals of the two end circles of one half torus differ by about `1.7e-12` (rounding at coordinates of about 200, minor radius 0.1). `smgu_IntersectTwoPlanes` treats planes as parallel only below `1e-12`, so it returns the line where they cross: it lies about `rounding / angle` = 0.0153 off the true axis. The common-axis test (`4e-8`) then refuses the torus. The two planes are the same plane to rounding; the angle tolerance (not `SM_EFF_ZERO`) is the right bound for taking them as parallel.
- **Trigger:** a fillet of radius 0.1 at coordinates of about 200 (an e-bike battery pack design in SolidCraft: 24 instances of the error).
- **How found:** SolidCraft reported the error when converting a body from its flat arrays (`crates/smlib/src/core/convert.rs`); the cause was traced to the two planes' normals.
- **Confirmed:** no. The pinned C++ is not built on this machine; the port reproduces it with the opt-in off: `small_far_half_torus_is_found_with_near_parallel_iso_planes` (`crates/solidcore/src/surface/torus.rs`).
- **Rust port:** opt-in `Kernel::fixes.iso_circle_planes_parallel_within_angle_tolerance` (default `false`, upstream's behaviour) in `test_for_iso_circles` (`crates/solidcore/src/surface/surf_of_revolution/p1.rs`). With it on, planes whose normals' cross product is below the angle tolerance and whose distance is within `tol_3d` are one plane and any other pair is intersected as before; with it off the call to `intersect_two_planes` is unchanged.
