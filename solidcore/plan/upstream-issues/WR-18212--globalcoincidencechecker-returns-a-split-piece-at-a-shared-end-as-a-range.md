#### WR-18212. `SmCurve::GlobalCoincidenceChecker` returns a split piece next to a shared end as a range, depending on the tolerance
- **Severity:** Wrong result
- **Area:** Curves
- **Location:** `SmCurve.cpp:19950`, `SmCurve::GlobalCoincidenceChecker`: after the end drops and the gap check, any two drops more than `SM_EFF_ZERO` apart in parameter are returned as a range.
- **What happens:** two curves that leave a shared end at an angle stay within `tol` of each other for a short way, about `tol / sin(angle)`. When one of them is split there (closed or half closed, e.g. a hook at its start), a split piece that lies inside that way without reaching the end has its own drops on the other curve and a gap under `tol`, so it is returned as a coincident range. Whether a piece falls inside depends on where the splits land, so a smaller tolerance can report a range that a larger one does not. A curve against itself also gets a second range that maps such a piece onto its neighbour piece.
- **Trigger:** edges e21 (a line) and e24 (a C0 cubic B-spline with a 1e-5 hook at its start) of face 9 of the base body of `blend.feature_executive` case `single_face_fillet1019_6`, which meet at about 38 degrees: ranges at `tol` 1.4e-3, 1.4897e-3, 2.5e-3, 4e-3 and 1e-2, none at 1e-3, 2e-3 or 2.889e-3 (SolidCraft issue #18212, `check_brep` face test #34). e24 against itself at 1.4e-3: e24 [1.35e-3, 2.24e-3] onto [2.24e-3, 3.57e-3] besides the real range.
- **How found:** `check_brep` face test #34 on the goldens case above (SolidCraft issue #18212).
- **Confirmed:** in the port, which follows `GlobalCoincidenceChecker` step for step here: the tests `issue_18212_edges_that_only_share_a_vertex_have_no_range_at_any_tolerance` and `issue_18212_a_curve_against_itself_is_one_range` (`crates/solidcore/src/curve/curve/p6.rs`) fail without the port's deviation. The C++ was not run on these curves.
- **Rust port:** on a split piece, a range whose stretch is one point at `tol` on either curve (tight box diagonal under `tol`) is not returned (`curve/curve/p6.rs`, module deviations). This covers curves meeting at about 24 degrees or more.
