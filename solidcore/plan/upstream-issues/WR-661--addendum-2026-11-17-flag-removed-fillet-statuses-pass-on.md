Updates: WR-661 (`WR-661--fillet-code-drops-statuses.md`)

#### WR-661 (update, 2026-11-17). `fillet_statuses_pass_on` is removed

The fillet executive, solvers, corners and geometry now always return the statuses SMLib drops and the fork passes on (tangency-labs/smlib 873c7c9, 66b02de, 9b7cd48). Propagation is the only path, and the flag is gone (tangency-labs/solidcraft#14246, group G1): the field, every ignore branch at the sites (`passed_on` with the flag, and the helpers left unused), `TemporaryRadiusChange`'s `*_with_fixes` variants, SolidCraft's preset line, its `fixes.json` entry and every test setting. The pass-on unit tests keep their pass-on half.

One site keeps a result (not upstream, which ignores this status for every body): `finish_merged_fillet`'s `OrientTrimmedSurfaces`. When the stitched result of an open input still has lamina edges, the closed-solid orientation's "gathered faceuses from both sides of some face" assert (`SmBrep.cpp`:24796, ported as `topology::brep::p3::GATHERED_BOTH_SIDES`) can fire on an open sheet that is orientable: walking a face use's neighbours crosses the sheet's boundary edges to its other side. The executive keeps the body only when it is the input sheet filleted (`blend::fillet_executive::session::finish::OpenInput`) and its own orientation succeeds:
  - every end vertex of every lamina edge of the result lies on the input's boundary;
  - every lamina edge on a face the input had runs along the input's boundary: the whole edge stays within the tolerance of one boundary piece, certified by `curves_stay_within` between the drops of its two ends (no sampled acceptance; a check of its middle point alone would take an edge that leaves the boundary between its ends). Only an edge whose faces the fillet made (a blend's end) may leave the boundary, with both ends on it. These two rules stand in for "every lamina edge was a lamina edge of the input", because the fillet rebuilds the edges it cuts back, so the result's boundary edges are new edges on the input's boundary curves. Face ids are never reused, so a face that is not among the input's was made by the fillet. A seam the stitch left unjoined, however far apart (the stitch has already retried up to 16 times the tolerance), is refused whenever one side is an input face, because its edge there runs off the boundary;
  - no lamina edge runs along another (a seam within the tolerance that the stitch did not join, such as between two faces the fillet made). A middle point that cannot be measured counts as on, so it refuses;
  - the open body's own orientation then succeeds: the one `StitchAndOrient` runs for a body with lamina edges (outward, just unifying the normals). It runs inside the exception, for every caller of this finish, `chamfer_fillet` included (`chamfer_fillet` runs `FilletSession` and the same finish path). The closed-solid pass that failed may have flipped faces before its check fired (`orient_ray_votes`, `faces_to_swap_uv`), so the kept body's normals are those of this unify, not that pass's. If the unify fails, the closed-solid failure is returned;
  - once the filleting and split are done, the kept body passes `check_brep` level 2. At orientation time the new edges are not yet validated, so level 2 cannot run there.

Otherwise the 1005 is returned and the body is restored. Every other orientation failure, and this one on a body without lamina edges, is passed on.

Not covered: a seam left unjoined by more than the tolerance between two faces that the fillet both made, with all its ends on the input's boundary. None of these rules refuses it, and neither does the unify or the level-2 check, which measure each edge and vertex against its own faces only, not against another lamina edge. It is kept out of reach by how a fillet builds, not refused: both sides would have to be fillet-made faces running rim to rim, so a blend (or patch) split lengthwise, and no fillet path splits one so before the finish orientation (ARGUED).
