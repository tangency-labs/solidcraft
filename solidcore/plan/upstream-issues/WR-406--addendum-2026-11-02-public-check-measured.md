Updates: WR-406

#### WR-406 (update, 2026-11-02). Gated at operation level through a bored plate and the whole-body check (#1954)

- **Why measured now.** The #1954 flag audit cites `uv_trim_reversal_on_surface` (a B-unit slice) with `solidcore::issue_891_kernel_fix_keeps_the_exact_trim` as its evidence. The tree's only test of that name is the unit test in `topology::brep::validation::pointers::issue_891_tests`, which calls `fix_reversed`, `fix_reversed_on` and `validate_pointers` on a hand-built face (it passes today; the module's 6 tests pass). It pins the trim repair itself but says nothing about what a caller's own check reports.
- **What now shows it.** `wr406_bored_plate_check_reverses_or_keeps_the_hole_trims` in `crates/solidcore/tests/wr406_uv_trim_reversal_check.rs` builds a body only through public operations: a 60 x 10 rectangle profile (`create_line_segment`) swept 4 up (`create_linear_sweep`), bored through the 4 thickness at (x, z) = (30, 2) by a radius-1 cylinder (`create_cylinder`, `transform`, `boolean_difference`). The bore's frame is turned 35 degrees about its own axis, so each hole trim's control polygon starts 35 degrees round the thin ellipse a 60 x 4 face's (u, v) makes of a radius-1 circle. Both trims are exact nine-point circles before the check, and the body is otherwise a default kernel: only `Kernel::fixes.uv_trim_reversal_on_surface` differs between the two runs.
- **The outcomes differ, measured:**

  | outcome | flag off (pinned upstream) | flag on (the fork's `PassesReverseDirectionCheckOn`) |
  |---|---|---|
  | body | 7 faces | the same 7 faces |
  | `check_brep(Level2)` `pointers_status` | 1000 (`SM_SUCCESS`) | 1000 |
  | `check_brep(Level2)` `assert_valid` | `Some(false)` | `Some(true)` |
  | failure lines | 2 x `SmBSplineCurve::Test #7 Bad CurveKink: 2D BSplineCurve Warning: Kink at interior knot (offset=-0.89764635, tolerance=0.9998477) [owner SmBSplineCurve]` | none |
  | hole trim, `EdgeuseId(80)`: middle control point before -> after | (0.5040929267989658, 0.1518178798399905) -> (0.5020464633994829, 0.32590893991999526) | (0.5040929267989658, 0.1518178798399905), unchanged |
  | hole trim, `EdgeuseId(86)`: middle control point before -> after | (0.4959070732010343, 0.1518178798399905) -> (0.4979535366005171, 0.32590893991999526) | (0.4959070732010343, 0.1518178798399905), unchanged |

- **The check changes the body it checks and then reports the change.** With the flag off, `ValidatePointers` (reached from `check_brep`) takes each exact trim's first-end turn for a reversal and moves that end's control point onto the chord (the exact midpoint of its neighbours, as `N_CrvIsNotReversed` does), and the following `AssertValid` reports the kink on the trim it just moved. With the flag on the same turn measured on the face's first fundamental form is square, the trims stay exact, and the check passes. This is the record's item 1 (`SmBrep::AssertValid` reporting test 7 on a body `ValidatePointers` had changed) reproduced through the public check; item 3 ("a check that writes") is confirmed, not altered.
- **Why this body folds in raw (u, v).** On the 60 x 4 faces the exact circle is an ellipse with 15:1 semi-axes, long along v. A quadratic control polygon vertex 10 degrees off the long axis's end turns back far enough in the raw (u, v) legs for the -0.95 cosine test, while the same turn measured with the surface's (E, F, G) there is square. An axis-aligned bore, by contrast, puts the polygon's vertices 45 degrees off those ends: that body measures identical in both flag states (7 faces, `pointers_status` 1000, `assert_valid` `Some(true)`, no lines, trims unchanged), so the flag's reach depends on where the trim's parameterisation starts on the ellipse, not on the operation alone. The committed test `wr406_axis_aligned_bore_keeps_the_check_clean` in `crates/solidcore/tests/wr406_uv_trim_reversal_check.rs` runs this control (same helpers, `turn = 0`, both flag states).
- **Mutation.** Forcing the flag's read in `crates/solidcore/src/topology/brep/validation/pointers.rs` on (`if true`) makes the off run keep its trims and pass, and the test fails at `assert_eq!(off.assert_valid, Some(false))` with `Some(true)`; forcing it off (`if false`) makes the on run straighten and report, and the test fails at `assert_eq!(on.assert_valid, Some(true))` with `Some(false)`. Restored, it passes.
- **Limits.** Measured in the Rust port only, not against the pinned C++ build; no tolerance, fixture or kernel behaviour changed, and the flag stays off by default. The bore's 35-degree turn is chosen to put a control-polygon vertex near the ellipse's long axis end (its natural purpose: a bore at an arbitrary orientation); the axis-aligned control above is the same body without that turn. The record's C++ location and line numbers are unchanged and not re-measured here.
